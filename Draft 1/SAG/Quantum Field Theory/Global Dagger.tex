\chapter{Hermitian Morita Theory and the Global Dagger}
\label{chap:hermitian-morita-global-dagger}

\section{Purpose and standing conventions}
\label{sec:hermitian-morita-purpose}

Fix
\[
    A\in\CAlg(\V),
    \qquad
    \mathcal W:=\Mod_A(\V).
\]
This chapter applies coherent homotopy-fixed-point constructions to the
canonical Morita duality of
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}.

The object-level operation is passage to the opposite algebra,
\[
    B\longmapsto B^{\op},
\]
and the canonical weak duality on the right-perfect Morita
$(\infty,2)$-category is
\[
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    =
    (M^R)^{\op}.
\]
A Hermitian associative algebra is a coherent fixed point of the
opposite-algebra operation. Its algebra equivalence
\[
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}
\]
determines a regular Morita equivalence between the same endpoints.
For fixed Hermitian endpoints, the canonical Morita duality therefore
transports to a coherent contravariant duality of the corresponding
right-perfect bimodule category.

A unitary bimodule is a coherent fixed point of this transported
duality. Its selected fixed-point equivalence identifies Hermitian action
reversal with the Morita right adjoint. Conjugating the transported
duality by these selected equivalences defines a dagger on every
bimodule map.

The chapter proceeds in two stages.

First, it develops the local theory:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item Hermitian associative algebras and their regular Morita fixed
    points;

    \item Hermitian action reversal of bimodules;

    \item transported Morita duality on fixed-endpoint hom categories;

    \item unitary right-perfect bimodules;

    \item object decoration and the local dagger.
\end{enumerate}

Second, the decorated hom categories and their coherent Morita
composition functors are assembled into a Segal object in
\[
    \Cat_\infty.
\]
Applying the functorial completion convention of
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}
produces a global $(\infty,2)$-category
\[
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}
\]
equipped with a coherent dagger involution.

The term \emph{Hermitian} continues to mean coherent
homotopy-fixed-point data. No involution of the commutative base, no
semilinear conjugation, and no positivity condition are imposed.

The mapping-space, higher-coherence, variance, universe, and completion
conventions of
\Ref{chap:perfect-rigid-enrichment},
\Ref{chap:hermitian-poincare},
and
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}
remain in force.

\begin{convention}[Hermitian and unitary fixed-point data]
\label{conv:hermitian-morita-fixed-points}
Every Hermitian or unitary object includes its complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-homotopy-fixed-point structure.

Displaying only the underlying equivalence and its first fixed-point
equation does not truncate the structure. Transport of fixed-point data
along equivalences, Morita composition, base change, object decoration,
and completion is always performed by the corresponding equivariant,
pseudofunctorial, or universal construction.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Action reversal and dagger notation]
\label{conv:hermitian-reversal-dagger-notation}
For a bimodule
\[
    M\colon B\longrightarrow C,
\]
the notation
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
\]
denotes Hermitian action reversal. It is a bimodule
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)\colon C\longrightarrow B.
\]

For every such bimodule, the canonical double-reversal equivalence is
denoted
\[
    \epsilon^J_M
    \colon
    J_{C,B}J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M.
\]

The symbol
\[
    \dagger
\]
is reserved for the involution on bimodule maps induced by selected
unitary structures.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Meaning of the global dagger]
\label{conv:global-dagger-meaning}
A \emph{global dagger} in this chapter means a coherent weak duality
involution
\[
    \dagger
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X
\]
equipped with its specified coherent trivializations on the represented
objects and represented $1$-morphisms.

Thus the dagger reverses $2$-morphisms, while the selected Hermitian and
unitary structures identify its action on represented objects and
$1$-morphisms with the identity.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Iterated duality notation]
\label{conv:hermitian-morita-iterated-duality}
For a coherent duality
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal C^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal C,
\]
we write
\[
    D^2
    :=
    DD^{\op}
    \colon
    \mathcal C
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C.
\]

For a coherent $1$-op involution
\[
    O
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X,
\]
we write
\[
    O^2
    :=
    OO^{\op}
    \colon
    \mathcal X
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal X.
\]

For a coherent weak duality
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X,
\]
we write
\[
    D^2
    :=
    DD^{\mathrm{co}}
    \colon
    \mathcal X
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal X.
\]

The same convention is used for the induced dualities on mapping
$\infty$-categories. Thus every occurrence of a square of a
contravariant or $2$-contravariant duality denotes the correctly typed
covariant composite.
\end{convention}

\section{Core actions of coherent dualities}
\label{sec:core-actions-coherent-dualities}

Two different core actions occur in this chapter.

The opposite-algebra operation is covariant on algebra maps:
\[
    u
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C
    \qquad\longmapsto\qquad
    u^{\op}
    \colon
    B^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    C^{\op}.
\]

By contrast, a contravariant duality of an $\infty$-category reverses
ordinary morphisms. Its induced action on the maximal
$\infty$-groupoid uses inversion.

\begin{lemma}[The core action of a coherent duality]
\label{lem:core-action-coherent-duality}
Let
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal C^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal C
\]
be an $\infty$-category with coherent duality and biduality
\[
    \operatorname{bid}
    \colon
    \id_{\mathcal C}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    DD^{\op}.
\]

There is a covariant coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-action
\[
    \sigma_D
    \colon
    \mathcal C^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C^\simeq
\]
defined on objects by
\[
    \sigma_D(X)=D(X)
\]
and on equivalences
\[
    f
    \colon
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    Y
\]
by
\[
    \sigma_D(f)
    :=
    D(f^{-1})
    \colon
    D(X)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(Y).
\]

A coherent fixed object has an underlying equivalence
\[
    s_X
    \colon
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(X).
\]
Its first fixed-point equation may be written equivalently as
\[
    D(s_X^{-1})\circ s_X
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}_X
\]
or as
\[
    D(s_X)\circ\operatorname{bid}_X
    \simeq
    s_X.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The action is the composite
\[
    \mathcal C^\simeq
    \xrightarrow{\operatorname{inv}}
    \bigl(
        \mathcal C^{\op}
    \bigr)^\simeq
    \xrightarrow{D^\simeq}
    \mathcal C^\simeq,
\]
where
\[
    \operatorname{inv}(f)=f^{-1}.
\]

Applying the action twice sends
\[
    X
\]
to
\[
    DD^{\op}(X)=D^2(X).
\]
The biduality equivalence
\[
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D^2(X)
\]
and its involutivity modification provide the first visible layers of
the coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-action. The remaining higher simplices are those of the coherent
duality on
\[
    \mathcal C.
\]

For a fixed point
\[
    s_X
    \colon
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(X),
\]
the ordinary cocycle equation is
\[
    \sigma_D(s_X)\circ s_X
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}_X.
\]
Since
\[
    \sigma_D(s_X)
    =
    D(s_X^{-1}),
\]
this is the first displayed equation.

Applying
\[
    D(s_X)
\]
on the left and using
\[
    D(s_X)\circ D(s_X^{-1})
    \simeq
    \id_{D(X)}
\]
gives
\[
    D(s_X)\circ\operatorname{bid}_X
    \simeq
    s_X.
\]
The converse follows by composing with
\[
    D(s_X^{-1}).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:weak-duality-object-core-action}
For a weak duality
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X
\]
of an $(\infty,2)$-category, the situation at the object level is
different.

The operation
\[
    (-)^{\mathrm{co}}
\]
does not reverse $1$-morphisms. Hence the induced action on the maximal
$\infty$-groupoid of objects and equivalence $1$-morphisms sends
\[
    u
    \colon
    x
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    y
\]
directly to
\[
    D(u)
    \colon
    D(x)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(y),
\]
without inserting
\[
    u^{-1}.
\]
\end{remark}

\section{Hermitian associative algebras}
\label{sec:hermitian-associative-algebras}

Passage to the opposite algebra defines a coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-action on
\[
    \Alg_A^\simeq.
\]

\begin{definition}[Hermitian associative algebra]
\label{def:hermitian-associative-algebra}
A \emph{Hermitian associative $A$-algebra} is a coherent homotopy fixed
point for the involution
\[
    B
    \longmapsto
    B^{\op}
\]
on
\[
    \Alg_A^\simeq.
\]

Its underlying equivalence is an equivalence of associative
$A$-algebras
\[
    \theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}.
\]
Its first fixed-point coherence is
\[
    \theta_B^{\op}\circ\theta_B
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{\op}_B,
\]
where
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{\op}_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op\op}
\]
is the canonical double-opposite equivalence.

Under the standard identification
\[
    B^{\op\op}
    \simeq
    B,
\]
this is written
\[
    \theta_B^{\op}\circ\theta_B
    \simeq
    \id_B.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The space of Hermitian associative algebras]
\label{def:space-hermitian-associative-algebras}
Define
\[
    \HermAlg_A
    :=
    \bigl(
        \Alg_A^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}.
\]
Thus
\[
    \HermAlg_A
\]
is the space of Hermitian associative $A$-algebras, Hermitian algebra
equivalences, and all coherent higher equivalences between them.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Anti-multiplicativity]
\label{rem:opposite-essential-noncommutative}
The map
\[
    \theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}
\]
encodes anti-multiplicativity.

If the operation is denoted symbolically by
\[
    x
    \longmapsto
    x^*,
\]
then
\[
    (xy)^*
    =
    y^*x^*.
\]

No involution of the commutative base is required. Since
\[
    A\in\CAlg(\V),
\]
there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    A^{\op}
    \simeq
    A.
\]
\end{remark}

\subsection{Regular Morita equivalences}

\begin{construction}[Regular bimodule of an algebra equivalence]
\label{con:regular-bimodule-algebra-equivalence}
Let
\[
    u
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C
\]
be an equivalence of associative $A$-algebras.

Define
\[
    U_u
    :=
    {}_B C_C
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
where the left $B$-action is induced by
\[
    u
\]
and the right $C$-action is multiplication.

Its inverse regular Morita equivalence is denoted
\[
    U_u^{-1}
    :=
    U_{u^{-1}}
    \colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    B.
\]
\end{construction}

\begin{lemma}[Regular algebra equivalences are Morita equivalences]
\label{lem:regular-algebra-equivalence-morita}
For every algebra equivalence
\[
    u
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C,
\]
the bimodule
\[
    U_u
\]
is a Morita equivalence.

There are canonical equivalences
\[
    U_u\otimes_CU_u^{-1}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    {}_B B_B
\]
and
\[
    U_u^{-1}\otimes_BU_u
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    {}_C C_C.
\]

The right adjoint of
\[
    U_u
\]
is canonically equivalent to
\[
    U_u^{-1}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The extension and restriction bimodules form an adjunction
\[
    {}_B C_C
    \dashv
    {}_C C_B.
\]
The unit is induced by
\[
    u
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
and the counit is multiplication
\[
    C\otimes_BC
    \longrightarrow
    C.
\]

Since
\[
    u
\]
is an equivalence of associative algebras, extension and restriction of
scalars are inverse equivalences. Hence the unit and counit are
equivalences.

The restriction bimodule
\[
    {}_C C_B
\]
is canonically equivalent to the regular bimodule associated to
\[
    u^{-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
    U_u^R
    \simeq
    U_u^{-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Equivariance of regular Morita equivalences]
\label{prop:regular-morita-equivariance}
The regular-bimodule construction defines a functor of spaces
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_A
    \colon
    \Alg_A^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \bigl(
        \Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}}
    \bigr)^\simeq
\]
which is the identity on objects and sends an algebra equivalence
\[
    u
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C
\]
to
\[
    U_u
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C.
\]

There is a canonical coherent equivariance equivalence
\[
    \mathbb D_A(U_u)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    U_{u^{\op}}.
\]
These comparisons are compatible with identity algebra maps,
composition of algebra equivalences, double opposites, the biduality of
\[
    \mathbb D_A,
\]
and the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By
\Ref{lem:regular-algebra-equivalence-morita},
the bimodule
\[
    U_u
\]
is right perfect and has right adjoint
\[
    U_u^R
    \simeq
    U_{u^{-1}}.
\]
Hence
\[
    \mathbb D_A(U_u)
    =
    (U_u^R)^{\op}
    \simeq
    (U_{u^{-1}})^{\op}.
\]

The action-reversed bimodule
\[
    (U_{u^{-1}})^{\op}
\]
is a
\[
    B^{\op}
    \text{--}
    C^{\op}
\]
-bimodule with underlying object
\[
    C.
\]
Its left
\[
    B^{\op}
\]
-action and right
\[
    C^{\op}
\]
-action are precisely the regular actions induced by
\[
    u^{\op}
    \colon
    B^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C^{\op}.
\]
Thus there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \mathbb D_A(U_u)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    U_{u^{\op}}.
\]

For composable algebra equivalences
\[
    B
    \xrightarrow{u}
    C
    \xrightarrow{v}
    D,
\]
multiplication induces the canonical regular-bimodule comparison
\[
    U_u\otimes_CU_v
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    U_{v\circ u}.
\]
Compatibility of the displayed equivariance equivalence with this
comparison is the compatibility of right-adjoint formation and action
reversal with Morita composition.

The compatibility with identities, double opposites, biduality, and all
higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences is inherited from the coherent mate and action-reversal
constructions of
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{construction}[The regular Morita fixed point]
\label{con:regular-morita-fixed-point}
Let
\[
    (B,\theta_B)
\]
be a Hermitian associative algebra.

Define
\[
    \Theta_B
    :=
    U_{\theta_B}
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}.
\]
Its inverse is
\[
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    :=
    U_{\theta_B^{-1}}
    \colon
    B^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B.
\]

By
\Ref{prop:regular-morita-equivariance},
the complete coherent fixed-point structure on
\[
    \theta_B
\]
transports to a complete coherent fixed-point structure on
\[
    \Theta_B
\]
for the object action of
\[
    \mathbb D_A.
\]

Its first fixed-point coherence is an invertible $2$-morphism
\[
    \mathbb D_A(\Theta_B)\circ\Theta_B
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \operatorname{bid}^{\mathbb D}_B.
\]
In Morita tensor notation, this is
\[
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(\Theta_B)
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \operatorname{bid}^{\mathbb D}_B.
\]

The same datum also makes
\[
    \Theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    O_A(B)=B^{\op}
\]
a coherent fixed object for the action on the object core induced by the
coherent $1$-op involution
\[
    O_A
    \colon
    \Mor_A^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Mor_A.
\]
Indeed, the core action of a $1$-op involution sends an equivalence
\[
    U
    \colon
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    Y
\]
to
\[
    O_A(U^{-1})
    \colon
    O_A(X)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    O_A(Y).
\]
Since
\[
    \Theta_B^R
    \simeq
    \Theta_B^{-1},
\]
one has
\[
    \mathbb D_A(\Theta_B)
    =
    O_A(\Theta_B^R)
    \simeq
    O_A(\Theta_B^{-1}).
\]
Thus the displayed fixed-point coherence is simultaneously the first
fixed-point coherence for the $1$-op core action. The complete higher
coherences agree because both structures are transported from the same
coherent opposite-algebra fixed point
\[
    \theta_B.
\]
\end{construction}

\begin{remark}[Algebraic and Morita fixed points]
\label{rem:hermitian-homotopy-fixed-points}
A Hermitian associative algebra is a coherent fixed object in the
$\infty$-groupoid of associative algebras and algebra equivalences.

The regular-bimodule construction sends it to a coherent fixed object of
the right-perfect Morita category. A general fixed object of the Morita
duality may instead be equipped only with a Morita equivalence
\[
    B
    \simeq
    B^{\op}.
\]
The present chapter retains the distinguished fixed objects arising from
equivalences of associative algebras.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Hermitian endomorphism algebras}

\begin{proposition}[Equivariance of the endomorphism-algebra construction]
\label{prop:endomorphism-algebra-equivariance}
The assignment
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    \End_A(M)
    :=
    \Perf(A)(M,M)
\]
defines a functor
\[
    \mathsf{End}_A
    \colon
    \Perf(A)^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \Alg_A^\simeq.
\]

It is equivariant for the coherent perfect duality
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    M^\vee
\]
on
\[
    \Perf(A)^\simeq
\]
and the opposite-algebra involution on
\[
    \Alg_A^\simeq.
\]

The equivariance comparison at
\[
    M
\]
is the algebra equivalence
\[
    \alpha_M
    \colon
    \End_A(M^\vee)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \End_A(M)^{\op}
\]
given by
\[
    \alpha_M(g)
    :=
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    g^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Enriched composition makes
\[
    \End_A(M)
    =
    \Perf(A)(M,M)
\]
an associative algebra object in
\[
    \Mod_A(\V).
\]

For an equivalence
\[
    u
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    N,
\]
define
\[
    \mathsf{End}_A(u)(f)
    :=
    u\circ f\circ u^{-1}.
\]
This is an equivalence of associative algebras. The coherent
functoriality follows from coherent composition in the enriched
category
\[
    \Perf(A).
\]

The enriched dualization construction of
\Ref{chap:perfect-rigid-enrichment}
gives a natural equivalence
\[
    d_{M,M}
    \colon
    \Perf(A)(M,M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Perf(A)(M^\vee,M^\vee)
\]
which reverses enriched composition. Equivalently, its inverse gives an
algebra equivalence
\[
    \alpha_M
    \colon
    \End_A(M^\vee)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \End_A(M)^{\op}.
\]

Unwinding enriched dualization gives
\[
    \alpha_M(g)
    =
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    g^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M.
\]

Naturality of enriched dualization, compatibility with biduality, and
the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structure on perfect duality supply the full equivariance of
\[
    \mathsf{End}_A.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitian endomorphism algebra]
\label{prop:hermitian-endomorphism-algebra}
Let
\[
    (M,h_M)
\]
be a Hermitian perfect $A$-module in the sense of
\Ref{chap:hermitian-poincare}. Then
\[
    \End_A(M)
    :=
    \Perf(A)(M,M)
    \simeq
    M^\vee\otimes_AM
\]
is naturally a Hermitian associative $A$-algebra.

Its underlying anti-involution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \theta_{\End(M)}
    \colon
    \End_A(M)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \End_A(M)^{\op},
    \\
    f
    &\longmapsto
    h_M^{-1}\circ f^\vee\circ h_M.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the equivariant functor
\[
    \mathsf{End}_A
\]
of
\Ref{prop:endomorphism-algebra-equivariance}
to the coherent fixed point
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^\vee.
\]
The underlying algebra equivalence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \theta_{\End(M)}
    \colon
    \End_A(M)
    &\xrightarrow{\mathsf{End}_A(h_M)}
    \End_A(M^\vee)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\alpha_M}
    \End_A(M)^{\op}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For
\[
    f
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    M,
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \theta_{\End(M)}(f)
    &=
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    \bigl(
        h_M\circ f\circ h_M^{-1}
    \bigr)^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M
    \\
    &\simeq
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    (h_M^{-1})^\vee
    \circ
    f^\vee
    \circ
    h_M^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M.
\end{aligned}
\]

The Hermitian fixed-point equation
\[
    h_M^\vee\circ\operatorname{bid}_M
    \simeq
    h_M
\]
implies
\[
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    (h_M^{-1})^\vee
    \simeq
    h_M^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
    \theta_{\End(M)}(f)
    \simeq
    h_M^{-1}\circ f^\vee\circ h_M.
\]

The complete coherent fixed-point structure is the image of the complete
fixed-point structure of
\[
    h_M
\]
under the equivariant functor
\[
    \mathsf{End}_A.
\]
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Equivariant endomorphism Morita context]
\label{prop:equivariant-endomorphism-morita-context}
For
\[
    M\in\Perf(A),
\]
write
\[
    B_M
    :=
    \End_A(M)
\]
and let
\[
    E_M
    :=
    {}_{B_M}M_A
    \colon
    B_M
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
be the evaluation bimodule.

Let
\[
    \mathfrak E_A
    :=
    \int_{B\in\Alg_A^\simeq}
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}}}(B,A)^\simeq
\]
denote the space obtained by unstraightening the functor which sends an
associative algebra
\[
    B
\]
to the maximal $\infty$-groupoid of right-perfect Morita morphisms
\[
    B
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
and transports along algebra equivalences by regular Morita
equivalences. Using the canonical Hermitian fixed point
\[
    A
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    A^{\op},
\]
canonical Morita duality induces a coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-action on
\[
    \mathfrak E_A.
\]

The assignment
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    (B_M,E_M)
\]
defines a functor of spaces
\[
    \mathsf{EndMor}_A
    \colon
    \Perf(A)^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \mathfrak E_A.
\]
For an equivalence
\[
    u
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    N,
\]
the endpoint component is conjugation
\[
    c_u
    \colon
    B_M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B_N,
    \qquad
    c_u(b)
    :=
    u\circ b\circ u^{-1},
\]
and the bimodule component is the map
\[
    u
    \colon
    {}_{B_M}M_A
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    U_{c_u}
    \otimes_{B_N}
    {}_{B_N}N_A.
\]

This functor is coherently equivariant for canonical perfect duality on
\[
    \Perf(A)^\simeq
\]
and canonical Morita duality on
\[
    \mathfrak E_A.
\]
Its endpoint comparison is
\[
    \alpha_M
    \colon
    B_{M^\vee}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B_M^{\op},
    \qquad
    \alpha_M(g)
    =
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    g^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M.
\]
Its Morita-morphism comparison is an equivalence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \eta_M
    \colon
    E_{M^\vee}
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    U_{\alpha_M}
    \otimes_{B_M^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(E_M).
\end{aligned}
\]
After identifying both sides with the underlying object
\[
    M^\vee\in\mathcal W,
\]
the map
\[
    \eta_M
\]
is the identity.

The comparisons
\[
    \alpha_M
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \eta_M
\]
are compatible with equivalences of perfect modules, biduality,
enriched composition, Morita right adjoints, and the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
    M\in\Perf(A),
\]
the evaluation bimodule
\[
    E_M
    \colon
    B_M
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
is right perfect. Its Morita right adjoint is
\[
    E_M^R
    =
    {}_AM^\vee_{B_M},
\]
where the right
\[
    B_M
\]
-action is precomposition:
\[
    \phi\cdot b
    :=
    \phi\circ b.
\]
Thus, after transport along the canonical equivalence
\[
    A^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    A,
\]
one may regard
\[
    \mathbb D_A(E_M)
    =
    (E_M^R)^{\op}
    \colon
    B_M^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
as a Morita morphism with underlying object
\[
    M^\vee.
\]

For an equivalence
\[
    u
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    N,
\]
the identity
\[
    u(bm)
    =
    c_u(b)u(m)
\]
shows that
\[
    u
\]
is a bimodule equivalence after transport of the left endpoint along
\[
    c_u.
\]
Compatibility with identities and composition follows from coherent
composition in the enriched category
\[
    \Perf(A).
\]
This constructs
\[
    \mathsf{EndMor}_A.
\]

The endpoint equivalence
\[
    \alpha_M
    \colon
    B_{M^\vee}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B_M^{\op}
\]
is the equivariance comparison of
\Ref{prop:endomorphism-algebra-equivariance}.
Transport
\[
    \mathbb D_A(E_M)
\]
along
\[
    \alpha_M.
\]
The resulting
\[
    B_{M^\vee}\text{--}A
\]
-bimodule is
\[
    U_{\alpha_M}
    \otimes_{B_M^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(E_M).
\]
Its underlying object is
\[
    M^\vee.
\]
Define
\[
    \eta_M
\]
to be the identity of this underlying object.

It remains to verify its left
\[
    B_{M^\vee}
\]
-linearity. Let
\[
    g\in B_{M^\vee}.
\]
On the transported target, the action of
\[
    g
\]
is induced by precomposition with
\[
    \alpha_M(g)
    =
    \operatorname{bid}_M^{-1}
    \circ
    g^\vee
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_M.
\]
Precomposition by an endomorphism
\[
    a
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    M
\]
acts on
\[
    M^\vee
\]
by
\[
    a^\vee.
\]
The involutivity coherence of perfect duality gives
\[
    \alpha_M(g)^\vee
    \simeq
    g.
\]
Consequently the transported action of
\[
    g
\]
on the target is the ordinary evaluation action of
\[
    g
\]
on
\[
    M^\vee.
\]
Hence
\[
    \eta_M
\]
is an equivalence of
\[
    B_{M^\vee}\text{--}A
\]
-bimodules.

The maps
\[
    d_{M,N}
\]
of enriched dualization assemble into an enriched contravariant
equivalence, reverse enriched composition, and induce the coherent mate
operation on parameterized morphisms. Therefore
\[
    \alpha_M
\]
and
\[
    \eta_M
\]
are natural in equivalences of perfect modules and compatible with the
Morita units, counits, and mate correspondences. Compatibility with
biduality and all higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences is inherited from the coherent perfect duality and the
coherent canonical Morita duality. Thus
\[
    \mathsf{EndMor}_A
\]
is coherently equivariant.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed-endpoint transport of weak duality}
\label{sec:fixed-endpoint-transport}

The transported duality on a Morita hom category is an instance of a
general construction for $(\infty,2)$-categories with weak duality.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-endpoint transport of weak duality]
\label{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport}
Let
\[
    \mathcal X
\]
be an $(\infty,2)$-category equipped with a coherent weak duality
involution
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X.
\]
Write
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}
    \colon
    \id_{\mathcal X}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D^2
\]
for its biduality.

Let
\[
    (x,\Theta_x)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    (y,\Theta_y)
\]
be coherent fixed objects for the induced action on the maximal
$\infty$-groupoid of objects and equivalence $1$-morphisms. Thus
\[
    \Theta_x
    \colon
    x
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(x),
    \qquad
    \Theta_y
    \colon
    y
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(y),
\]
and the first fixed-point coherences are
\[
    D(\Theta_x)\circ\Theta_x
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_x,
\]
\[
    D(\Theta_y)\circ\Theta_y
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_y.
\]

Then the mapping $\infty$-category
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y)
\]
carries a canonical coherent duality
\[
    D_{x,y}
    \colon
    \mathcal X(x,y)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X(x,y).
\]
For a $1$-morphism
\[
    f
    \colon
    x
    \longrightarrow
    y,
\]
it is
\[
    D_{x,y}(f)
    :=
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    D(f)
    \circ
    \Theta_x.
\]
For a $2$-morphism
\[
    \alpha
    \colon
    f
    \Longrightarrow
    g,
\]
the corresponding morphism
\[
    \alpha^{\op}
    \colon
    g
    \longrightarrow
    f
\]
in
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y)^{\op}
\]
is sent to the whiskering of
\[
    D(\alpha^{\mathrm{co}})
    \colon
    D(g)
    \Longrightarrow
    D(f)
\]
with
\[
    \Theta_x
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \Theta_y^{-1}.
\]

There is a coherent biduality equivalence
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{x,y}
    \colon
    \id_{\mathcal X(x,y)}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D_{x,y}D_{x,y}^{\op}.
\]
Its inverse at
\[
    f
\]
is the canonical composite
\[
\begin{aligned}
    D_{x,y}^2(f)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{D}_y
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_x
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    f.
\end{aligned}
\]

The construction is natural in equivalences of coherent fixed objects.
After passage to maximal $\infty$-groupoids, the coherent duality induces
the corresponding covariant coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-action by inversion as in
\Ref{lem:core-action-coherent-duality}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
    \mathcal X^\simeq
\]
denote the maximal $\infty$-groupoid of objects and equivalence
$1$-morphisms of
\[
    \mathcal X.
\]
Composition with equivalence $1$-morphisms defines a functor
\[
    \underline{\Hom}_{\mathcal X}
    \colon
    (\mathcal X^\simeq)^{\op}
    \times
    \mathcal X^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \Cat_\infty,
\]
whose value at
\[
    (x,y)
\]
is
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y).
\]
For equivalences
\[
    u
    \colon
    x'
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    x,
    \qquad
    v
    \colon
    y
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    y',
\]
the induced transport is
\[
    f
    \longmapsto
    v\circ f\circ u.
\]

The weak duality
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal X
\]
induces coherent semilinear equivalences
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X(Dx,Dy).
\]
The biduality, involutivity modification, and higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-cocycle make these equivalences compatible with the induced action on
the endpoint space
\[
    \mathcal X^\simeq.
\]

The coherent fixed objects
\[
    (x,\Theta_x)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    (y,\Theta_y)
\]
provide a coherent equivariant lift of the point
\[
    (x,y)
    \in
    (\mathcal X^\simeq)^{\op}
    \times
    \mathcal X^\simeq.
\]
Pulling back the semilinear hom construction along this fixed endpoint
diagram produces a homotopy-fixed-point structure for the
opposite-category action on
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y).
\]
Equivalently, it produces a coherent duality
\[
    D_{x,y}
    \colon
    \mathcal X(x,y)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X(x,y).
\]
Its nontrivial functor is the composite
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal X(x,y)^{\op}
    &\xrightarrow{D}
    \mathcal X(Dx,Dy)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \Theta_y^{-1}\circ(-)\circ\Theta_x
    }
    \mathcal X(x,y),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the displayed formula.

It remains to identify the visible biduality. Applying the functor twice
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    D_{x,y}^2(f)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    D(\Theta_y^{-1})
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    D(\Theta_x)
    \circ
    \Theta_x.
\end{aligned}
\]
The fixed-point coherences imply
\[
    D(\Theta_x)\circ\Theta_x
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_x
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Theta_y^{-1}\circ D(\Theta_y^{-1})
    &\simeq
    \bigl(
        D(\Theta_y)\circ\Theta_y
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \\
    &\simeq
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{D}_y
    \bigr)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
    D_{x,y}^2(f)
    \simeq
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{D}_y
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_x.
\]
Pseudonaturality of
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}
\]
gives
\[
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_x
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \operatorname{bid}^{D}_y
    \circ
    f.
\]
Hence there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    D_{x,y}^2(f)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    f.
\]
Its inverse is
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{x,y}_f.
\]
The involutivity modification and all higher cocycle simplices are those
of the pulled-back fixed endpoint diagram.

For naturality, let
\[
    u
    \colon
    (x',\Theta_{x'})
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (x,\Theta_x),
\]
\[
    v
    \colon
    (y,\Theta_y)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (y',\Theta_{y'})
\]
be equivalences of coherent fixed objects. Their first equivariance
conditions are
\[
    D(u)\circ\Theta_{x'}
    \simeq
    \Theta_x\circ u,
\]
\[
    D(v)\circ\Theta_y
    \simeq
    \Theta_{y'}\circ v.
\]
The transport functor
\[
    T_{u,v}
    \colon
    \mathcal X(x,y)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal X(x',y')
\]
is
\[
    T_{u,v}(f)
    :=
    v\circ f\circ u.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
    D_{x',y'}
    \bigl(
        T_{u,v}(f)
    \bigr)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_{y'}^{-1}
    \circ
    D(v)
    \circ
    D(f)
    \circ
    D(u)
    \circ
    \Theta_{x'}
    \\
    &\simeq
    v
    \circ
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    D(f)
    \circ
    \Theta_x
    \circ
    u
    \\
    &=
    T_{u,v}
    \bigl(
        D_{x,y}(f)
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The complete naturality coherence is inherited from the same pulled-back
fixed endpoint diagrams.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of fixed-endpoint transport]
\label{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-functoriality}
Let
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X
\]
and
\[
    E
    \colon
    \mathcal Y^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal Y
\]
be coherent weak duality involutions.

Let
\[
    F
    \colon
    \mathcal X
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal Y
\]
be an $(\infty,2)$-functor equipped with a pseudonatural adjoint
equivalence
\[
    \kappa
    \colon
    E\circ F^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F\circ D
\]
compatible with the bidualities, involutivity modifications, and complete
coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.

If
\[
    (x,\Theta_x)
\]
is a coherent fixed object of
\[
    D,
\]
then
\[
    F(x)
\]
has the transported fixed-point structure
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Theta_{F(x)}
    \colon
    F(x)
    &\xrightarrow{F(\Theta_x)}
    F(Dx)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\kappa_x^{-1}}
    E(Fx).
\end{aligned}
\]

For coherent fixed objects
\[
    x
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    y,
\]
the induced hom functor
\[
    F_{x,y}
    \colon
    \mathcal X(x,y)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal Y(Fx,Fy)
\]
is a morphism of the transported coherent dualities. There is a canonical
natural equivalence
\[
    \overline\kappa_f
    \colon
    E_{F(x),F(y)}
    \bigl(
        F(f)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F
    \bigl(
        D_{x,y}(f)
    \bigr)
\]
compatible with composition, biduality, and the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-point structure on
\[
    F(x)
\]
is the image of the fixed point
\[
    \Theta_x
\]
under the equivariant functor
\[
    (F,\kappa).
\]
Its complete fixed-point coherence follows from the compatibility of
\[
    \kappa
\]
with the weak-duality structures.

For a morphism
\[
    f
    \colon
    x
    \longrightarrow
    y,
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
    E_{F(x),F(y)}
    \bigl(
        F(f)
    \bigr)
    &=
    \Theta_{F(y)}^{-1}
    \circ
    E(F(f))
    \circ
    \Theta_{F(x)}
    \\
    &\simeq
    F(\Theta_y^{-1})
    \circ
    F(D(f))
    \circ
    F(\Theta_x)
    \\
    &=
    F
    \bigl(
        D_{x,y}(f)
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle equivalence is obtained from the pseudonaturality of
\[
    \kappa.
\]

The compatibility with all $2$-morphisms, composition, biduality, and the
higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences follows by applying
\[
    F
\]
and
\[
    \kappa
\]
to the pulled-back
\[
    BC_2
\]
-diagrams used in
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport}.
\end{proof}


\section{Fixed-endpoint transport of a coherent $1$-op involution}
\label{sec:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op}

Hermitian action reversal is the fixed-endpoint transport of the
coherent $1$-op involution on the Morita category. We first construct it
in an arbitrary $(\infty,2)$-category.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-endpoint transport of a coherent $1$-op involution]
\label{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op}
Let
\[
    O
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X
\]
be a coherent $1$-op involution with biduality
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}
    \colon
    \id_{\mathcal X}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    OO^{\op}.
\]
Its induced action on the maximal $\infty$-groupoid of objects and
equivalence $1$-morphisms is
\[
    \sigma_O
    :=
    O^\simeq\circ\operatorname{inv}.
\]

Let
\[
    (x,\Theta_x)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    (y,\Theta_y)
\]
be coherent fixed objects for this action. Thus
\[
    \Theta_x
    \colon
    x
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    O(x),
    \qquad
    \Theta_y
    \colon
    y
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    O(y),
\]
and the first fixed-point equations are
\[
    O(\Theta_x^{-1})\circ\Theta_x
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}_x,
\]
\[
    O(\Theta_y^{-1})\circ\Theta_y
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}_y.
\]

Then there is a canonical functor
\[
    J_{x,y}
    \colon
    \mathcal X(x,y)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X(y,x)
\]
defined by
\[
    J_{x,y}(f)
    :=
    \Theta_x^{-1}
    \circ
    O(f)
    \circ
    \Theta_y.
\]
For composable $1$-morphisms
\[
    x
    \xrightarrow{f}
    y
    \xrightarrow{g}
    z,
\]
there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \zeta_{f,g}
    \colon
    J_{x,z}(g\circ f)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    J_{x,y}(f)
    \circ
    J_{y,z}(g).
\]
These equivalences are coherently associative and unital.

For every
\[
    f
    \colon
    x
    \longrightarrow
    y,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
    \epsilon^J_f
    \colon
    J_{y,x}J_{x,y}(f)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    f.
\]
Its underlying comparison is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{y,x}J_{x,y}(f)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{O}_y
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \circ
    O^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}_x
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    f.
\end{aligned}
\]
The compositors and double-reversal comparisons satisfy the complete
coherent involutivity relations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $1$-op involution induces coherent semilinear equivalences
\[
    \mathcal X(x,y)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X(Oy,Ox).
\]
Pulling this semilinear hom construction back along the coherent fixed
endpoint data produces the displayed functor
\[
    J_{x,y}.
\]
This pullback already supplies the complete composition, unit,
involutivity, and higher coherence data. We identify the visible
comparisons.

For composable
\[
    f
    \colon
    x
    \longrightarrow
    y,
    \qquad
    g
    \colon
    y
    \longrightarrow
    z,
\]
contravariance of
\[
    O
\]
on $1$-morphisms gives
\[
    O(g\circ f)
    \simeq
    O(f)\circ O(g).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{x,z}(g\circ f)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_x^{-1}
    \circ
    O(f)
    \circ
    O(g)
    \circ
    \Theta_z
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Theta_x^{-1}
    \circ
    O(f)
    \circ
    \Theta_y
    \circ
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    O(g)
    \circ
    \Theta_z
    \\
    &=
    J_{x,y}(f)
    \circ
    J_{y,z}(g).
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle equivalence inserts the inverse-equivalence unit for
\[
    \Theta_y.
\]

For double reversal,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{y,x}J_{x,y}(f)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    O
    \bigl(
        \Theta_x^{-1}
        \circ
        O(f)
        \circ
        \Theta_y
    \bigr)
    \circ
    \Theta_x
    \\
    &\simeq
    \Theta_y^{-1}
    \circ
    O(\Theta_y)
    \circ
    O^2(f)
    \circ
    O(\Theta_x^{-1})
    \circ
    \Theta_x.
\end{aligned}
\]
The fixed-point equations and their inverses give
\[
    \Theta_y^{-1}\circ O(\Theta_y)
    \simeq
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{O}_y
    \bigr)^{-1},
\]
\[
    O(\Theta_x^{-1})\circ\Theta_x
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}_x.
\]
Hence
\[
    J_{y,x}J_{x,y}(f)
    \simeq
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{O}_y
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \circ
    O^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}_x.
\]
Pseudonaturality of
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{O}
\]
identifies this with
\[
    f.
\]
The complete coherent relations are inherited from the pulled-back
homotopy-fixed-point construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of $1$-op fixed-endpoint transport]
\label{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op-functoriality}
Let
\[
    O_{\mathcal X}
    \colon
    \mathcal X^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal X,
    \qquad
    O_{\mathcal Y}
    \colon
    \mathcal Y^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal Y
\]
be coherent $1$-op involutions.

Let
\[
    F
    \colon
    \mathcal X
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal Y
\]
be equipped with a pseudonatural adjoint equivalence
\[
    \omega
    \colon
    O_{\mathcal Y}
    \circ
    F^{\op}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F
    \circ
    O_{\mathcal X}
\]
compatible with the complete coherent $1$-op involution structures.

If
\[
    \Theta_x
    \colon
    x
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    O_{\mathcal X}(x)
\]
is a coherent fixed object, then
\[
    F(x)
\]
has the transported fixed-point structure
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Theta_{F(x)}
    \colon
    F(x)
    &\xrightarrow{F(\Theta_x)}
    F(O_{\mathcal X}x)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\omega_x^{-1}}
    O_{\mathcal Y}(Fx).
\end{aligned}
\]

For coherent fixed objects
\[
    x
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    y,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
    j_{F,f}
    \colon
    J^{\mathcal Y}_{F(x),F(y)}
    \bigl(
        F(f)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F
    \bigl(
        J^{\mathcal X}_{x,y}(f)
    \bigr).
\]
These comparisons are compatible with reversal of composition,
double reversal, equivalence $2$-morphisms, and the complete coherent
involutivity data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-point structure is the image of
\[
    \Theta_x
\]
under the equivariant functor
\[
    (F,\omega).
\]
For
\[
    f
    \colon
    x
    \longrightarrow
    y,
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J^{\mathcal Y}_{F(x),F(y)}
    \bigl(
        F(f)
    \bigr)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_{F(x)}^{-1}
    \circ
    O_{\mathcal Y}(Ff)
    \circ
    \Theta_{F(y)}
    \\
    &\simeq
    F(\Theta_x^{-1})
    \circ
    \omega_x
    \circ
    O_{\mathcal Y}(Ff)
    \circ
    \omega_y^{-1}
    \circ
    F(\Theta_y).
\end{aligned}
\]
Pseudonaturality of
\[
    \omega
\]
gives
\[
    F(O_{\mathcal X}f)
    \circ
    \omega_y
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \omega_x
    \circ
    O_{\mathcal Y}(Ff).
\]
Substitution yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J^{\mathcal Y}_{F(x),F(y)}
    \bigl(
        F(f)
    \bigr)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F(\Theta_x^{-1})
    \circ
    F(O_{\mathcal X}f)
    \circ
    F(\Theta_y)
    \\
    &=
    F
    \bigl(
        J^{\mathcal X}_{x,y}(f)
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is
\[
    j_{F,f}.
\]
All stated coherences follow by applying the equivariant functor to the
fixed-endpoint pullback construction of
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op}.
\end{proof}

\section{Hermitian reversal of bimodules}
\label{sec:hermitian-reversal-bimodules}

Let
\[
    (B,\theta_B)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be Hermitian associative $A$-algebras, with regular Morita fixed points
\[
    \Theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op},
\]
\[
    \Theta_C
    \colon
    C
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C^{\op}.
\]

\begin{construction}[Hermitian action reversal]
\label{con:hermitian-action-reversal}
Let
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C
\]
be a $B$--$C$-bimodule.

Define its \emph{Hermitian reverse} by
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{B,C}(M)
    &:=
    \Theta_C
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    M^{\op}
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    \\
    &\colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    B.
\end{aligned}
\]

On bimodule maps,
\[
    J_{B,C}
\]
is induced by ordinary action reversal and whiskering with
\[
    \Theta_C
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \Theta_B^{-1}.
\]
Thus there is a functor
\[
    J_{B,C}
    \colon
    \BMod_{B,C}(\mathcal W)
    \longrightarrow
    \BMod_{C,B}(\mathcal W).
\]
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Action-restriction description]
\label{prop:hermitian-reversal-action-restriction}
The bimodule
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
\]
has the same underlying object of
\[
    \mathcal W
\]
as
\[
    M.
\]

Its left
\[
    C
\]
-action is obtained from the left
\[
    C^{\op}
\]
-action on
\[
    M^{\op}
\]
by restriction along
\[
    \theta_C
    \colon
    C
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C^{\op}.
\]

Its right
\[
    B
\]
-action is obtained from the right
\[
    B^{\op}
\]
-action on
\[
    M^{\op}
\]
by restriction along
\[
    \theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The regular Morita equivalence
\[
    \Theta_C
    =
    {}_C C^{\op}_{C^{\op}}
\]
performs restriction of the left
\[
    C^{\op}
\]
-action along
\[
    \theta_C.
\]
Similarly,
\[
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    =
    {}_{B^{\op}}B_B
\]
performs restriction of the right
\[
    B^{\op}
\]
-action along
\[
    \theta_B.
\]

The relative tensor products with these regular equivalences do not
change the underlying object of
\[
    \mathcal W.
\]
They only transport the endpoint actions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coherent Hermitian reversal]
\label{prop:coherent-hermitian-reversal}
For composable bimodules
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
    \qquad
    N
    \colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    D
\]
between Hermitian associative algebras, there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \zeta_{M,N}
    \colon
    J_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    J_{C,D}(N)
    \otimes_C
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]

For every bimodule
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
    \epsilon^J_M
    \colon
    J_{C,B}J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M.
\]

The equivalences
\[
    \zeta
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \epsilon^J
\]
are coherently associative and unital and satisfy
\[
    J(\epsilon^J_M)
    \simeq
    \epsilon^J_{J(M)}
    \colon
    J^3(M)
    \longrightarrow
    J(M)
\]
together with all higher involutivity coherences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By
\Ref{prop:op-reversal-morita-composition},
there is a coherent equivalence
\[
    (M\otimes_CN)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    N^{\op}
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    M^{\op}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    &=
    \Theta_D
    \otimes_{D^{\op}}
    (M\otimes_CN)^{\op}
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    \\
    &\simeq
    \Theta_D
    \otimes_{D^{\op}}
    N^{\op}
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    M^{\op}
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Insert the canonical inverse-equivalence unit
\[
    \id_{C^{\op}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Theta_C^{-1}
    \otimes_C
    \Theta_C
\]
between
\[
    N^{\op}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    M^{\op}.
\]
Reassociation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \bigl(
        \Theta_D
        \otimes_{D^{\op}}
        N^{\op}
        \otimes_{C^{\op}}
        \Theta_C^{-1}
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\qquad\otimes_C
    \bigl(
        \Theta_C
        \otimes_{C^{\op}}
        M^{\op}
        \otimes_{B^{\op}}
        \Theta_B^{-1}
    \bigr)
    \\
    &=
    J_{C,D}(N)
    \otimes_C
    J_{B,C}(M).
\end{aligned}
\]
This defines
\[
    \zeta_{M,N}.
\]

Applying Hermitian reversal twice gives a bimodule whose underlying
object is
\[
    M^{\op\op}.
\]
Its endpoint actions are transported by the double fixed-point
composites
\[
    \theta_B^{\op}\circ\theta_B
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \theta_C^{\op}\circ\theta_C.
\]
The coherent fixed-point structures identify these composites with the
canonical double-opposite equivalences. Together with
\[
    M^{\op\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M,
\]
they define
\[
    \epsilon^J_M.
\]

Equivalently, the entire family
\[
    J_{B,C}
\]
is the fixed-endpoint transport of the coherent $1$-op involution
\[
    O_A
    \colon
    \Mor_A^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Mor_A
\]
along the coherent $1$-op fixed endpoints
\[
    \Theta_B
\]
constructed in
\Ref{con:regular-morita-fixed-point}.
The complete associativity, unit, involutivity, and higher coherences
therefore follow from
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hermitian reversal of an adjunction]
\label{lem:hermitian-reversal-adjunction}
Let
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \rightleftarrows
    C
    \colon
    N
\]
be an adjunction
\[
    M\dashv N
\]
in
\[
    \Mor_A.
\]
Then Hermitian reversal gives an adjunction
\[
    J_{C,B}(N)
    \dashv
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]

The construction is coherent in adjunctions, mates, and composition of
adjunctions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Ordinary action reversal sends
\[
    M\dashv N
\]
to
\[
    N^{\op}\dashv M^{\op}.
\]
Whiskering an adjunction on either side by equivalence
$1$-morphisms preserves the adjunction. Hence
\[
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    N^{\op}
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \Theta_C^{-1}
\]
is left adjoint to
\[
    \Theta_C
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    M^{\op}
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}.
\]
These are
\[
    J_{C,B}(N)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]

The coherent compatibility with units, counits, mates, and composition
is inherited from the coherent action-reversal involution and the
coherent mate calculus.
\end{proof}

\section{Transported Morita duality}
\label{sec:transported-morita-duality}

Let
\[
    \mathcal H_{B,C}
\]
denote the $\infty$-category of right-perfect
$B$--$C$-bimodules and all bimodule maps.

The canonical weak Morita duality of
\Ref{thm:weak-morita-duality-involution}
restricts to
\[
    \mathbb D_A
    \colon
    \mathcal H_{B,C}^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_{B^{\op},C^{\op}}.
\]

\begin{construction}[Transported Morita duality]
\label{con:transported-morita-duality}
Define
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}
    \colon
    \mathcal H_{B,C}^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_{B,C}
\]
by fixed-endpoint transport:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    &:=
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \Theta_C^{-1}
    \\
    &=
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    (M^R)^{\op}
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \Theta_C^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \simeq
    J_{C,B}(M^R).
\]

For a bimodule map
\[
    \alpha
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    N,
\]
define
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(\alpha)
    :=
    J_{C,B}(\alpha^R)
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(N)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M).
\]
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Coherent transported hom duality]
\label{prop:coherent-transported-hom-duality}
The functor
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}
    \colon
    \mathcal H_{B,C}^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal H_{B,C}
\]
is a coherent duality.

There is a natural biduality equivalence
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}
    \colon
    \id_{\mathcal H_{B,C}}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}\mathbb D_{B,C}^{\op}
\]
with its complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structure.

For composable right-perfect bimodules
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
    \qquad
    N
    \colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    D,
\]
there is a canonical equivalence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mu^{B,C,D}_{M,N}
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \otimes_C
    \mathbb D_{C,D}(N).
\end{aligned}
\]
These equivalences are coherently associative and unital.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The regular Morita equivalences
\[
    \Theta_B
    \colon
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op},
\]
\[
    \Theta_C
    \colon
    C
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C^{\op}
\]
make
\[
    B
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    C
\]
coherent fixed objects for the object action of
\[
    \mathbb D_A.
\]

Apply
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport}
to
\[
    \mathcal X
    =
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}},
    \qquad
    D
    =
    \mathbb D_A.
\]
This gives a coherent duality on
\[
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}}}(B,C)
    =
    \mathcal H_{B,C}.
\]

Its underlying functor is
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \Theta_C^{-1}.
\]
Since
\[
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    =
    (M^R)^{\op},
\]
this is
\[
    J_{C,B}(M^R).
\]

The coherent biduality and all higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences are those of fixed-endpoint transport.

For composition, begin with
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    &=
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(M\otimes_CN)
    \otimes_{D^{\op}}
    \Theta_D^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The global Morita-duality compositor gives
\[
    \mathbb D_A(M\otimes_CN)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(N).
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    \otimes_{C^{\op}}
    \mathbb D_A(N)
    \otimes_{D^{\op}}
    \Theta_D^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Insert
\[
    \id_{C^{\op}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Theta_C^{-1}\otimes_C\Theta_C
\]
between
\[
    \mathbb D_A(M)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \mathbb D_A(N).
\]
After reassociation this becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &
    \bigl(
        \Theta_B
        \otimes_{B^{\op}}
        \mathbb D_A(M)
        \otimes_{C^{\op}}
        \Theta_C^{-1}
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\qquad\otimes_C
    \bigl(
        \Theta_C
        \otimes_{C^{\op}}
        \mathbb D_A(N)
        \otimes_{D^{\op}}
        \Theta_D^{-1}
    \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \otimes_C
    \mathbb D_{C,D}(N).
\]

The associativity and unit coherences are inherited from the global
Morita-duality compositor and the fixed-point coherences of the
endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Right adjoint of the transported dual]
\label{lem:transported-dual-right-adjoint}
For every right-perfect
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
    \lambda_M
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)^R
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Morita adjunction
\[
    M
    \dashv
    M^R
\]
is converted by
\Ref{lem:hermitian-reversal-adjunction}
into
\[
    J_{C,B}(M^R)
    \dashv
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]
The left-hand side is
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M).
\]
Thus
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
\]
is a right adjoint of
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M).
\]

The coherent uniqueness of right adjoints supplies
\[
    \lambda_M
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)^R
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]
Naturality and compatibility with composition follow from the coherent
mate calculus.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Explicit transported biduality]
\label{prop:explicit-transported-biduality}
For every right-perfect
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
the inverse of the transported biduality is canonically equivalent to
the composite
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \bigl(
        \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,C}^2(M)
    &=
    J_{C,B}
    \bigl(
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)^R
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        J_{C,B}(\lambda_M)
    }
    J_{C,B}J_{B,C}(M)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\epsilon^J_M}
    M.
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
    \simeq
    J_{C,B}(\lambda_M^{-1})
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Begin with the specified Morita adjunction
\[
    M
    \dashv
    M^R.
\]
Hermitian reversal gives the specified adjunction
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    =
    J_{C,B}(M^R)
    \dashv
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]

Thus, before forgetting specified adjunction data, the selected right
adjoint of
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
\]
is
\[
    J_{B,C}(M).
\]
The comparison with the coherently selected right adjoint is
\[
    \lambda_M.
\]

Applying Hermitian reversal once more and then applying
\[
    \epsilon^J_M
    \colon
    J_{C,B}J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M
\]
gives the displayed composite
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}^2(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M.
\]

On the specified-adjunction resolution, this is the visible double
mate-reversal comparison. The fixed-endpoint transport construction
identifies it with the inverse of
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Unitary right-perfect bimodules}
\label{sec:unitary-right-perfect-bimodules}

\begin{definition}[Unitary right-perfect bimodule]
\label{def:unitary-right-perfect-bimodule}
Let
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C
\]
be right perfect.

A \emph{unitary structure} on
\[
    M
\]
is a coherent homotopy-fixed-point structure for the duality
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}
    \colon
    \mathcal H_{B,C}^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal H_{B,C}.
\]

Its underlying equivalence is
\[
    s_M
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M),
\]
and its first fixed-point coherence is
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(s_M)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
    \simeq
    s_M.
\]

A \emph{unitary right-perfect bimodule} is a pair
\[
    (M,s_M)
\]
equipped with this complete coherent fixed-point structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Adjoint formulation of unitarity]
\label{prop:unitary-adjoint-formulation}
Let
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C
\]
be right perfect.

There are natural equivalences of spaces
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi_M,\Psi_M
    \colon
    \Map^\simeq_{\mathcal H_{B,C}}
    \bigl(
        M,
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Map^\simeq_{\BMod_{C,B}(\mathcal W)}
    \bigl(
        J_{B,C}(M),
        M^R
    \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
defined by
\[
    \Phi_M(s)
    :=
    s^R\circ\lambda_M^{-1}
\]
and
\[
    \Psi_M(s)
    :=
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
    \circ
    J_{B,C}(s).
\]

The inverse of
\[
    \Psi_M
\]
is
\[
    \Psi_M^{-1}(u)
    =
    J_{C,B}(u)
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}.
\]

Let
\[
    t_M(s)
    :=
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(s)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
\]
be transposition on
\[
    \Map^\simeq_{\mathcal H_{B,C}}
    \bigl(
        M,
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr).
\]
Then
\[
    t_M
    \simeq
    \Psi_M^{-1}\circ\Phi_M.
\]

Transporting
\[
    t_M
\]
through
\[
    \Psi_M
\]
defines a coherent involution
\[
    T_M
    :=
    \Phi_M\circ\Psi_M^{-1}
\]
on
\[
    \Map^\simeq_{\BMod_{C,B}(\mathcal W)}
    \bigl(
        J_{B,C}(M),
        M^R
    \bigr).
\]

Consequently, a unitary structure on
\[
    M
\]
is equivalently an equivalence
\[
    u_M
    \colon
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R
\]
equipped with a coherent homotopy-fixed-point structure for
\[
    T_M.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Taking right mates is an equivalence of mapping spaces by
\Ref{prop:mate-equivalence}. Since
\[
    \lambda_M
\]
is an equivalence,
\[
    s
    \longmapsto
    s^R\circ\lambda_M^{-1}
\]
defines the equivalence
\[
    \Phi_M.
\]

Hermitian reversal and
\[
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
\]
are equivalences. Therefore
\[
    \Psi_M(s)
    =
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
    \circ
    J_{B,C}(s)
\]
also defines an equivalence of spaces.

Let
\[
    u
    \colon
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R.
\]
Define
\[
    s_u
    :=
    J_{C,B}(u)
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M).
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Psi_M(s_u)
    &=
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
    \circ
    J_{B,C}
    \left(
        J_{C,B}(u)
        \circ
        \bigl(
            \epsilon^J_M
        \bigr)^{-1}
    \right)
    \\
    &\simeq
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
    \circ
    J^2(u)
    \circ
    J
    \left(
        \bigl(
            \epsilon^J_M
        \bigr)^{-1}
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Naturality of
\[
    \epsilon^J
\]
gives
\[
    \epsilon^J_{M^R}
    \circ
    J^2(u)
    \simeq
    u\circ\epsilon^J_{J(M)}.
\]
The involutivity coherence gives
\[
    \epsilon^J_{J(M)}
    \simeq
    J(\epsilon^J_M).
\]
Therefore
\[
    \Psi_M(s_u)
    \simeq
    u.
\]
This proves the displayed formula for
\[
    \Psi_M^{-1}.
\]

By
\Ref{prop:explicit-transported-biduality},
\[
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
    \simeq
    J_{C,B}(\lambda_M^{-1})
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}.
\]
On maps, the transported duality is
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(s)
    =
    J_{C,B}(s^R).
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    t_M(s)
    &=
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(s)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,C}_M
    \\
    &\simeq
    J_{C,B}(s^R)
    \circ
    J_{C,B}(\lambda_M^{-1})
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \\
    &=
    J_{C,B}
    \bigl(
        s^R\circ\lambda_M^{-1}
    \bigr)
    \circ
    \bigl(
        \epsilon^J_M
    \bigr)^{-1}
    \\
    &=
    \Psi_M^{-1}\Phi_M(s).
\end{aligned}
\]

Transporting the complete coherent involution
\[
    t_M
\]
through
\[
    \Psi_M
\]
gives
\[
    T_M
    =
    \Phi_M\Psi_M^{-1}.
\]
The corresponding homotopy-fixed-point spaces are therefore equivalent.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Meaning of the adjoint formulation]
\label{rem:unitary-identifies-duality-adjoint}
A unitary structure identifies two independently defined
$C$--$B$-bimodules:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item the Hermitian reverse
    \[
        J_{B,C}(M),
    \]
    obtained from the anti-involutions of the source and target
    algebras;

    \item the Morita right adjoint
    \[
        M^R,
    \]
    obtained from right perfectness.
\end{enumerate}

The coherent
\[
    T_M
\]
-fixed-point structure is the mate symmetry which makes
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R
\]
a unitary comparison rather than an arbitrary equivalence.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Closure under Morita composition]
\label{thm:unitary-bimodule-composition}
Let
\[
    (M,s_M)
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
    \qquad
    (N,s_N)
    \colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    D
\]
be unitary right-perfect bimodules.

Then
\[
    M\otimes_CN
\]
has a canonical unitary right-perfect structure.

Its underlying fixed-point equivalence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{M\otimes N}
    \colon
    M\otimes_CN
    &\xrightarrow{
        s_M\otimes_Cs_N
    }
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \otimes_C
    \mathbb D_{C,D}(N)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \bigl(
            \mu^{B,C,D}_{M,N}
        \bigr)^{-1}
    }
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the adjoint formulation, if
\[
    u_M
    \colon
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R
\]
and
\[
    u_N
    \colon
    J_{C,D}(N)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    N^R
\]
are the corresponding mate-symmetric equivalences, then the composite
unitary comparison is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    J_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        M\otimes_CN
    \bigr)
    &\xrightarrow{\zeta_{M,N}}
    J_{C,D}(N)
    \otimes_C
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        u_N\otimes_Cu_M
    }
    N^R\otimes_CM^R
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \chi_{M,N}^{-1}
    }
    (M\otimes_CN)^R.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Right perfectness follows from
\Ref{prop:morita-adjoint-composite}.

The Morita composition functor
\[
    -\otimes_C-
    \colon
    \mathcal H_{B,C}
    \times
    \mathcal H_{C,D}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_{B,D}
\]
is a morphism of coherent dualities through the comparison
\[
    \mu^{B,C,D}.
\]

Passing to maximal $\infty$-groupoids gives a
\[
    C_2
\]
-equivariant map
\[
    \mathcal H_{B,C}^\simeq
    \times
    \mathcal H_{C,D}^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_{B,D}^\simeq.
\]
Taking homotopy fixed points gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \bigl(
        \mathcal H_{B,C}^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}
    \times
    \bigl(
        \mathcal H_{C,D}^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}
    \longrightarrow
    \bigl(
        \mathcal H_{B,D}^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The image of
\[
    (s_M,s_N)
\]
under this map has underlying equivalence
\[
    s_{M\otimes N}.
\]
Its complete higher fixed-point data are supplied by the equivariant
composition construction.

The adjoint formula follows from
\Ref{prop:coherent-hermitian-reversal},
\Ref{prop:coherent-adjoints-composites},
and the equivalence of the two formulations in
\Ref{prop:unitary-adjoint-formulation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical coherent unitary identity bimodule]
\label{prop:unit-bimodule-unitary}
For every Hermitian associative algebra
\[
    (B,\theta_B),
\]
the regular identity bimodule
\[
    \mathbf 1_B
    :=
    {}_B B_B
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    B
\]
has a canonical unitary right-perfect structure
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_B}
    \colon
    \mathbf 1_B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,B}(\mathbf 1_B).
\]

These unitary identity structures are natural in equivalences of
Hermitian associative algebras. More precisely, if
\[
    u
    \colon
    (B,\theta_B)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
is a Hermitian algebra equivalence, then transport by the corresponding
unitary regular Morita equivalence identifies
\[
    (\mathbf 1_B,s_{\mathbf 1_B})
\]
with
\[
    (\mathbf 1_C,s_{\mathbf 1_C}).
\]
The identity structures are coherently compatible with composition and
identity Hermitian algebra equivalences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity bimodule is right adjoint to itself and is therefore right
perfect. The unit comparison of the global Morita duality gives a
coherent equivalence
\[
    \mathbb D_A(\mathbf 1_B)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbf 1_{B^{\op}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb D_{B,B}(\mathbf 1_B)
    &\simeq
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \mathbf 1_{B^{\op}}
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    \\
    &\simeq
    \Theta_B
    \otimes_{B^{\op}}
    \Theta_B^{-1}
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbf 1_B.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
    c_B
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{B,B}(\mathbf 1_B)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbf 1_B
\]
denote this contraction and define
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_B}
    :=
    c_B^{-1}.
\]

The global unit comparison for
\[
    \mathbb D_A,
\]
the inverse-equivalence triangle data for
\[
    \Theta_B,
\]
and the coherent fixed-point structure of
\[
    \Theta_B
\]
identify
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,B}(s_{\mathbf 1_B})
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}^{B,B}_{\mathbf 1_B}
\]
with
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_B}.
\]
All higher fixed-point coherences are inherited from the equivariant unit
section of the transported Morita composition system. Hence
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_B}
\]
is a complete unitary structure.

Let
\[
    u
    \colon
    (B,\theta_B)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be a Hermitian algebra equivalence. Transport along the regular Morita
equivalence
\[
    U_u
\]
sends the underlying identity bimodule to the identity bimodule through
the unit constraints
\[
    U_u^{-1}
    \otimes_B
    \mathbf 1_B
    \otimes_B
    U_u
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbf 1_C.
\]
The regular-bimodule equivariance and the fixed-point path represented by
\[
    u
\]
intertwine
\[
    c_B
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    c_C.
\]
Therefore the displayed transport carries
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_B}
\]
to
\[
    s_{\mathbf 1_C}.
\]
Compatibility with composition and identities is inherited from the
coherent regular-bimodule functor and the Morita unit constraints.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Plain Hermitian reversal need not preserve right perfectness]
\label{rem:plain-reversal-not-right-perfect}
The Hermitian reverse
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
\]
of a right-perfect bimodule need not itself be right perfect.

A unitary structure identifies it with
\[
    M^R,
\]
but a right adjoint need not itself admit a right adjoint.

Accordingly, the canonical weak duality on the right-perfect Morita
category is
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    (M^R)^{\op},
\]
not plain action reversal.
\end{remark}

\section{Object decoration and local dagger categories}
\label{sec:object-decoration-local-dagger}

The unitary structure is imposed on objects of a hom category. Morphisms
are not required to preserve the selected unitary structures. Instead,
the selected structures define the dagger of every underlying bimodule
map.

\begin{construction}[Object decoration of an $\infty$-category]
\label{con:object-decoration}
Let
\[
    \mathcal C
\]
be an $\infty$-category and let
\[
    e
    \colon
    E
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C^\simeq
\]
be a map of spaces.

Define a simplicial space
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E)_n
    :=
    \Fun([n],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    \times_{
        (\mathcal C^\simeq)^{n+1}
    }
    E^{n+1},
\]
where
\[
    \Fun([n],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    \longrightarrow
    (\mathcal C^\simeq)^{n+1}
\]
is evaluation at the vertices.

Let
\[
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E)
\]
denote the $\infty$-category presented by the functorial completion of
this Segal space, using the completion convention of
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Properties and functoriality of object decoration]
\label{prop:object-decoration-properties}
The simplicial space
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E)_\bullet
\]
is a Segal space.

Its represented objects are points
\[
    x\in E.
\]
For
\[
    x,y\in E,
\]
there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \Map_{\Dec(\mathcal C,E)}(x,y)
    \simeq
    \Map_{\mathcal C}
    \bigl(
        e(x),
        e(y)
    \bigr).
\]

The forgetful functor
\[
    q
    \colon
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C
\]
is fully faithful. Its essential image is the full subcategory of
objects lying in the essential image of
\[
    e.
\]

The construction is functorial as follows. Let
\[
    F
    \colon
    \mathcal C
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal D
\]
be a functor, let
\[
    e
    \colon
    E
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C^\simeq,
    \qquad
    e'
    \colon
    E'
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal D^\simeq,
\]
and let
\[
    \phi
    \colon
    E
    \longrightarrow
    E'
\]
be a map equipped with a coherent homotopy
\[
    e'\circ\phi
    \simeq
    F^\simeq\circ e.
\]
Then there is an induced functor
\[
    \Dec(F,\phi)
    \colon
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E)
    \longrightarrow
    \Dec(\mathcal D,E').
\]

These functors are coherently compatible with identities and
composition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The categorical nerve of
\[
    \mathcal C
\]
satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Fun([n],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    \simeq
    \underbrace{
        \Fun([1],\mathcal C)^\simeq
        \times_{\mathcal C^\simeq}
        \cdots
        \times_{\mathcal C^\simeq}
        \Fun([1],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    }_{n\text{ factors}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Pulling this equivalence back along
\[
    E^{n+1}
    \longrightarrow
    (\mathcal C^\simeq)^{n+1}
\]
gives the Segal equivalence for
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E)_\bullet.
\]

Before completion, the mapping space from
\[
    x
\]
to
\[
    y
\]
is the fibre of
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E)_1
    \longrightarrow
    E\times E
\]
over
\[
    (x,y).
\]
This fibre is
\[
    \Map_{\mathcal C}
    \bigl(
        e(x),
        e(y)
    \bigr).
\]

Functorial completion is a Dwyer--Kan equivalence and preserves the
represented mapping spaces. Hence
\[
    \Map_{\Dec(\mathcal C,E)}(x,y)
    \simeq
    \Map_{\mathcal C}
    \bigl(
        e(x),
        e(y)
    \bigr).
\]
The forgetful functor is therefore fully faithful. Its essential image
is the full subcategory of objects equivalent to points in the image of
\[
    e.
\]

The pair
\[
    (F,\phi)
\]
defines in every degree a map
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &
    \Fun([n],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    \times_{(\mathcal C^\simeq)^{n+1}}
    E^{n+1}
    \\
    &\qquad\longrightarrow
    \Fun([n],\mathcal D)^\simeq
    \times_{(\mathcal D^\simeq)^{n+1}}
    (E')^{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These maps commute with all simplicial operators. Functoriality of
completion gives
\[
    \Dec(F,\phi).
\]
Identity and composition coherences are inherited degreewise.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Products of object decorations]
\label{prop:object-decoration-products}
Let
\[
    e
    \colon
    E
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C^\simeq,
    \qquad
    e'
    \colon
    E'
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal D^\simeq
\]
be maps of spaces.

There is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \Dec
    \bigl(
        \mathcal C\times\mathcal D,
        E\times E'
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E)
    \times
    \Dec(\mathcal D,E').
\]

These equivalences are coherently associative, unital, and symmetric.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every
\[
    n\geq0,
\]
there is a canonical equivalence
\[
    \Fun
    \bigl(
        [n],
        \mathcal C\times\mathcal D
    \bigr)^\simeq
    \simeq
    \Fun([n],\mathcal C)^\simeq
    \times
    \Fun([n],\mathcal D)^\simeq.
\]
The vertex-evaluation maps are products of the corresponding evaluation
maps. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &
    \widetilde{\Dec}
    \bigl(
        \mathcal C\times\mathcal D,
        E\times E'
    \bigr)_n
    \\
    &\qquad\simeq
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E)_n
    \times
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal D,E')_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the uncompleted decorated Segal spaces agree degreewise.

Products of Dwyer--Kan equivalences are Dwyer--Kan equivalences, and
products of complete Segal spaces are complete. Therefore the product of
the two completions is a completion of the product Segal space.
The universal property of functorial completion gives the displayed
equivalence.

All associativity, unit, and symmetry coherences are inherited from
finite products in spaces and
\[
    \Cat_\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-point decoration and dagger]
\label{prop:fixed-point-decoration-dagger}
Let
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal C^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal C
\]
be an $\infty$-category with coherent duality.

Let
\[
    E_D
    :=
    (\mathcal C^\simeq)^{hC_2},
\]
where the
\[
    C_2
\]
-action on
\[
    \mathcal C^\simeq
\]
is
\[
    \sigma_D
    =
    D^\simeq\circ\operatorname{inv}
\]
as in
\Ref{lem:core-action-coherent-duality}.
Let
\[
    e_D
    \colon
    E_D
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal C^\simeq
\]
be evaluation at the underlying object.

Then
\[
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E_D)
\]
carries a canonical coherent dagger
\[
    \dagger
    \colon
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E_D)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E_D).
\]
It fixes represented objects with their specified coherent
identifications. For fixed objects
\[
    (X,s_X),
    \qquad
    (Y,s_Y),
\]
and a morphism
\[
    f
    \colon
    X
    \longrightarrow
    Y,
\]
it is
\[
    f^\dagger
    :=
    s_X^{-1}
    \circ
    D(f)
    \circ
    s_Y
    \colon
    Y
    \longrightarrow
    X.
\]
It satisfies
\[
    (g\circ f)^\dagger
    \simeq
    f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger,
\]
\[
    (f^\dagger)^\dagger
    \simeq
    f,
\]
\[
    \id_X^\dagger
    \simeq
    \id_X,
\]
together with all coherent higher involutivity data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The homotopy-fixed-point space
\[
    E_D
\]
carries the universal fixed-point equivalence
\[
    s
    \colon
    e_D
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \sigma_D\circ e_D.
\]
Its component at
\[
    (X,s_X)
\]
is the selected equivalence
\[
    s_X
    \colon
    X
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(X).
\]

An $n$-simplex of
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E_D)
\]
is represented by a chain
\[
    X_0
    \xrightarrow{f_1}
    X_1
    \xrightarrow{f_2}
    \cdots
    \xrightarrow{f_n}
    X_n
\]
together with coherent fixed-point structures
\[
    s_{X_i}
    \colon
    X_i
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    D(X_i).
\]
Define its dagger to be the reversed chain
\[
    X_n
    \xrightarrow{f_n^\dagger}
    X_{n-1}
    \longrightarrow
    \cdots
    \xrightarrow{f_1^\dagger}
    X_0,
\]
where
\[
    f_i^\dagger
    :=
    s_{X_{i-1}}^{-1}
    \circ
    D(f_i)
    \circ
    s_{X_i}.
\]
This operation is obtained by applying the order-reversal equivalence
\[
    [n]^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
    [n],
\]
then applying
\[
    D,
\]
and finally conjugating at the vertices by the universal fixed-point
equivalence
\[
    s.
\]

For composable morphisms
\[
    X
    \xrightarrow{f}
    Y
    \xrightarrow{g}
    Z,
\]
contravariance gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    (g\circ f)^\dagger
    &\simeq
    s_X^{-1}
    \circ
    D(f)
    \circ
    D(g)
    \circ
    s_Z
    \\
    &=
    f^\dagger
    \circ
    g^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the degreewise construction commutes with the inner face maps.
Compatibility with outer faces is restriction of the chain, and
compatibility with degeneracies follows from
\[
    \id_X^\dagger
    \simeq
    \id_X.
\]
Hence there is a morphism of Segal spaces
\[
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E_D)^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    \widetilde{\Dec}(\mathcal C,E_D).
\]

For involutivity, the first fixed-point equation is
\[
    D(s_X)\circ\operatorname{bid}_X
    \simeq
    s_X.
\]
Therefore
\[
    D(s_X)
    \simeq
    s_X\circ\operatorname{bid}_X^{-1}
\]
and
\[
    D(s_X^{-1})
    \simeq
    \operatorname{bid}_X\circ s_X^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    D(f^\dagger)
    &\simeq
    D(s_Y)
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    D(s_X^{-1})
    \\
    &\simeq
    s_Y
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_Y^{-1}
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_X
    \circ
    s_X^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Naturality of biduality gives
\[
    \operatorname{bid}_Y^{-1}
    \circ
    D^2(f)
    \circ
    \operatorname{bid}_X
    \simeq
    f.
\]
Hence
\[
    D(f^\dagger)
    \simeq
    s_Y\circ f\circ s_X^{-1},
\]
and therefore
\[
    (f^\dagger)^\dagger
    \simeq
    f.
\]

Because the degreewise construction is formed from the universal
complete fixed-point datum, its involutivity modification and all higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences commute with every simplicial operator. Functorial completion
therefore yields the coherent dagger on
\[
    \Dec(\mathcal C,E_D).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of fixed-point decoration]
\label{prop:fixed-point-decoration-functoriality}
Let
\[
    D
    \colon
    \mathcal C^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal C,
    \qquad
    E
    \colon
    \mathcal D^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal D
\]
be coherent dualities.

Let
\[
    F
    \colon
    \mathcal C
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal D
\]
be equipped with a natural equivalence
\[
    \rho
    \colon
    E\circ F^{\op}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F\circ D
\]
compatible with biduality and the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.

Then
\[
    F
\]
induces a functor
\[
    \Dec(F,\rho)
    \colon
    \Dec
    \left(
        \mathcal C,
        (\mathcal C^\simeq)^{hC_2}
    \right)
    \longrightarrow
    \Dec
    \left(
        \mathcal D,
        (\mathcal D^\simeq)^{hC_2}
    \right)
\]
which is compatible with the daggers through a coherent natural
equivalence
\[
    \Dec(F,\rho)\circ\dagger_{\mathcal C}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \dagger_{\mathcal D}
    \circ
    \Dec(F,\rho)^{\op}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivariant functor
\[
    (F,\rho)
\]
induces a map of homotopy-fixed-point spaces
\[
    \bigl(
        \mathcal C^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}
    \longrightarrow
    \bigl(
        \mathcal D^\simeq
    \bigr)^{hC_2}.
\]
Together with
\[
    F,
\]
this defines a functor of decorated categories by
\Ref{prop:object-decoration-properties}.

For a morphism
\[
    f
    \colon
    X
    \longrightarrow
    Y,
\]
the image of the dagger is
\[
    F
    \bigl(
        s_X^{-1}D(f)s_Y
    \bigr).
\]
The selected fixed-point structures on
\[
    F(X)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    F(Y)
\]
are obtained from
\[
    F(s_X),
    \qquad
    F(s_Y),
\]
and
\[
    \rho_X^{-1},
    \qquad
    \rho_Y^{-1}.
\]
Pseudonaturality of
\[
    \rho
\]
identifies the preceding expression with the dagger of
\[
    F(f).
\]

The complete compatibility follows by applying the equivariant functor
to the degreewise reversed-chain construction in
\Ref{prop:fixed-point-decoration-dagger}.
\end{proof}

\end{aligned}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply

and
\Ref{prop:fixed-point-decoration-dagger}

    \mathcal C
    =

    \qquad
    D

\end{proof}

\end{proposition}

\[
    q_{B,C}

\[

    \longrightarrow
    K_{B,C}
\]
over
\[
    M

\[
    M.

Hence the essential image is
\[
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{uadm}}_{B,C}.
\]
\end{proof}

\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Hermitian algebra equivalences give unitary Morita equivalences]
\label{lem:hermitian-equivalence-unitary-regular}
Let
\[
    u
    \colon
    (B,\theta_B)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be an equivalence in
\[
    \HermAlg_A.
\]

Then the regular extension bimodule
\[
    U_u
    =
    {}_B C_C
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C
\]
is a Morita equivalence and carries a canonical unitary structure
\[
    s_u
    \colon
    U_u
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(U_u).
\]

Under the action-restriction description of
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(U_u),
\]
the underlying $A$-module map of
\[
    s_u
\]
is represented by
\[
    \theta_C
    \colon
    C
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    C^{\op}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The bimodule
\[
    U_u
\]
is a Morita equivalence by
\Ref{lem:regular-algebra-equivalence-morita}.

By
\Ref{prop:regular-morita-equivariance},
the regular-bimodule assignment is equivariant for the
opposite-algebra action and the canonical Morita duality.

The equivalence
\[
    u
\]
is a path in the homotopy-fixed-point space
\[
    \HermAlg_A.
\]
Its first equivariance homotopy is
\[
    \theta_C\circ u
    \simeq
    u^{\op}\circ\theta_B
\]
as algebra maps
\[
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C^{\op}.
\]
Applying the equivariant regular-bimodule functor gives the unitary
fixed-point equivalence
\[
    s_u
    \colon
    U_u
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(U_u).
\]

The underlying object of both bimodules is
\[
    C.
\]
The displayed equivariance homotopy and anti-multiplicativity of
\[
    \theta_C
\]
show that the map represented by
\[
    \theta_C
\]
intertwines the transported endpoint actions.

The complete unitary coherence is the image of the complete coherent
fixed-point path
\[
    u
\]
under the equivariant regular-bimodule construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of unitary hom categories]
\label{prop:unitary-hom-category-functor}
The assignments
\[
    \bigl(
        (B,\theta_B),
        (C,\theta_C)
    \bigr)
    \longmapsto
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
\]
define a functor
\[
    \mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
    \colon
    (\HermAlg_A)^{\op}
    \times
    \HermAlg_A
    \longrightarrow
    \Cat_\infty.
\]

For equivalences
\[
    u
    \colon
    (B',\theta_{B'})
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (B,\theta_B)
\]
and
\[
    v
    \colon
    (C,\theta_C)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    (C',\theta_{C'}),
\]
the induced transport functor is represented by
\[
\begin{aligned}
    T_{u,v}
    \colon
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
    &\longrightarrow
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B',C'},
    \\
    M
    &\longmapsto
    U_u\otimes_BM\otimes_CU_v.
\end{aligned}
\]

The transport functors are compatible with the local daggers through
canonical coherent natural equivalences
\[

    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \dagger_{B',C'}
    \circ
    T_{u,v}^{\op}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On underlying right-perfect bimodule categories, fixed-endpoint
transport gives
\[
    T_{u,v}(M)
    :=
    U_u\otimes_BM\otimes_CU_v.
\]
Since
\[
    U_u
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    U_v
\]
are Morita equivalences, this preserves right-perfect bimodules.

Naturality in coherent fixed endpoints, proved in
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport},
makes
\[
    T_{u,v}
\]
a morphism of the transported coherent dualities. Applying
\Ref{prop:fixed-point-decoration-functoriality}
gives the functor on unitary hom categories and its compatibility with
the dagger.

For composable endpoint equivalences, the canonical regular-bimodule
equivalences
\[
    U_{u_2\circ u_1}
    \simeq
    U_{u_1}\otimes U_{u_2}

and their target analogues identify the corresponding transport
functors. The associativity and unit coherences are those of Morita
composition and the regular-bimodule functor.

Thus the assignment is a coherent functor
\[
    (\HermAlg_A)^{\op}
    \times
    \HermAlg_A
    \longrightarrow
    \Cat_\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Completion and variance operations]

\[

denote the functorial completion of Segal objects used in
\Ref{chap:right-perfect-morita-duality}.

\[
    \varepsilon
    \in
    \{\op,\mathrm{co},\mathrm{coop}\},
\]
and every Segal object
\[
    X_\bullet,
\]

\[
    L
    \bigl(
        X_\bullet^{\varepsilon}
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    L(X_\bullet)^{\varepsilon}.
\]
These equivalences are coherently compatible with the involutive
relations among
\[
    \op,
    \qquad
    \mathrm{co},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{coop}.
\]
Consequently, coherent actions involving any of these variance
operations pass canonically to functorial completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each variance operation is an autoequivalence of the $\infty$-category
of Segal objects in
\[
    \Cat_\infty.
\]
It preserves the Segal condition, complete objects, and
Dwyer--Kan equivalences.

The completion map
\[
    X_\bullet
    \longrightarrow
    L(X_\bullet)
\]
is a Dwyer--Kan equivalence with complete target. Applying
\[
    (-)^\varepsilon
\]
gives a Dwyer--Kan equivalence
\[
    X_\bullet^\varepsilon
    \longrightarrow
    L(X_\bullet)^\varepsilon
\]
with complete target. Hence
\[
    L(X_\bullet)^\varepsilon
\]
has the universal property of a completion of
\[
    X_\bullet^\varepsilon.
\]
Functorial uniqueness of completion supplies the displayed canonical
equivalence.

The relevant spaces of comparison equivalences are contractible.
Therefore the comparisons are coherently compatible with
\[
    \varepsilon^2\simeq\id
\]
and with
\[
    \op\circ\mathrm{co}
    \simeq
    \mathrm{coop}.
\]
Applying
\[
    L
\]
to a coherent variance-twisted
\[
    BC_2
\]
-diagram produces the corresponding coherent diagram after completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global dagger structure]
\label{thm:global-hermitian-dagger}
The local dagger functors
\[
    \dagger_{B,C}
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
\]
assemble into a coherent weak duality involution
\[
    \dagger
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}
    \bigr)^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}.
\]
It fixes the represented Hermitian objects and represented unitary
$1$-morphisms with their specified coherent identifications.

For a $2$-morphism
\[
    \alpha
    \colon
    (M,s_M)
    \longrightarrow
    (N,s_N)
\]
between unitary right-perfect
\[
    B\text{--}C
\]
-bimodules, it is
\[
    \alpha^\dagger
    :=
    s_M^{-1}
    \circ
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(\alpha)
    \circ
    s_N
    \colon
    N
    \longrightarrow
    M.
\]
It satisfies
\[
    (\beta\circ\alpha)^\dagger
    \simeq
    \alpha^\dagger\circ\beta^\dagger,
\]
\[
    (\alpha^\dagger)^\dagger
    \simeq
    \alpha,
\]
\[
    \id_M^\dagger
    \simeq
    \id_M,
\]
and, for horizontally composable maps,
\[
    (\alpha\otimes_C\beta)^\dagger
    \simeq
    \alpha^\dagger\otimes_C\beta^\dagger.
\]

For every selected unitary $1$-morphism
\[
    (M,s_M),
\]
the adjoint formulation gives a coherent mate-symmetric equivalence
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
    S
    :=
    \HermAlg_A.
\]
Since
\[
    S
\]
is an $\infty$-groupoid, inversion gives the canonical equivalence
\[
    S^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    S.
\]
Under this equivalence, define the degree-zero dagger
\[
    \dagger_0
    \colon
    \mathcal N_{A,0}^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal N_{A,0}
\]
to be the identity on represented Hermitian algebras and their complete
fixed-point data.

The functor of unitary hom categories is
\[
    \mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
    \colon
    S^{\op}\times S
    \longrightarrow
    \Cat_\infty,
\]
and its unstraightening is
\[
    (s,t)
    \colon
    \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
    \longrightarrow
    S\times S.
\]
For every pair
\[
    (B,C),
\]
\Ref{prop:unitary-hom-category-properties} supplies a coherent dagger
\[
    \dagger_{B,C}
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}.
\]
By
\Ref{prop:unitary-hom-category-functor},
these daggers are coherently natural in Hermitian equivalences of both
endpoints.

Since
\[
    S\times S
\]
is an $\infty$-groupoid, the opposite of the total unstraightening is
the unstraightening of the fiberwise opposite functor. Unstraightening
the preceding natural family therefore gives an equivalence
\[
    \dagger_1
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
\]
over the identity of
\[
    S\times S.
\]
It fixes represented decorated objects and sends every represented
bimodule map to its local dagger.

For
\[
    n\geq2,
\]
one has
\[
    \mathcal N_{A,n}
    =
    \underbrace{
        \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
        \times_S
        \cdots
        \times_S
        \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
    }_{n\text{ factors}}.
\]
The opposite-category functor preserves these limits. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \bigl(
        \mathcal N_{A,n}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \simeq
    \underbrace{
        \bigl(
            \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
        \bigr)^{\op}
        \times_S
        \cdots
        \times_S
        \bigl(
            \mathcal A_A^{\mathrm{unit}}
        \bigr)^{\op}
    }_{n\text{ factors}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Define
\[
    \dagger_n
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \mathcal N_{A,n}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal N_{A,n}
\]
by applying
\[
    \dagger_1
\]
to every factor. Because the desired global variance is
\[
    \mathrm{co},
\]
the order of the represented string of $1$-morphisms is unchanged; only
morphisms inside the hom $\infty$-categories are reversed.

We verify compatibility with the simplicial operators. Outer face maps
forget factors and therefore commute with
\[
    \dagger_n.
\]
Degeneracy maps insert the coherently natural unitary identities of
\Ref{prop:unit-bimodule-unitary},
so they also commute with the dagger.

For an inner face, let
\[
    \alpha
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    M',
    \qquad
    \beta
    \colon
    N
    \longrightarrow
    N'
\]
be horizontally composable maps. By definition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    (\alpha\otimes_C\beta)^\dagger
    &:=
    s_{M\otimes N}^{-1}
    \circ
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        \alpha\otimes_C\beta
    \bigr)
    \circ
    s_{M'\otimes N'}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Morita-duality compositor identifies
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,D}
    \bigl(
        \alpha\otimes_C\beta
    \bigr)
\]
with
\[
    \mathbb D_{B,C}(\alpha)
    \otimes_C
    \mathbb D_{C,D}(\beta).
\]
The composite unitary structures satisfy
\[
    s_{M\otimes N}
    =
    \mu_{M,N}^{-1}
    \circ
    (s_M\otimes_Cs_N).
\]
Substitution cancels the two occurrences of
\[
    \mu,
\]
and gives
\[
    (\alpha\otimes_C\beta)^\dagger
    \simeq
    \alpha^\dagger
    \otimes_C
    \beta^\dagger.
\]
The same argument in every arity uses the coherent multi-compositors and
shows that the family
\[
    \dagger_\bullet
\]
commutes with all inner face maps and all higher simplicial coherence
cells.

Thus there is a coherent equivalence of Segal objects
\[
    \dagger_\bullet
    \colon
    \mathcal N_{A,\bullet}^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \mathcal N_{A,\bullet}.
\]
The local involutivity modifications assemble factorwise. Every higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherence is inherited from the universal unitary fixed-point data and
the complete coherent transported Morita dualities.

Applying functorial completion and
\Ref{lem:completion-coopposite}
gives
\[
    \dagger
    \colon
    \bigl(
        \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}
    \bigr)^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}.
\]
The local formulas imply the displayed vertical composition,
involutivity, and identity laws. Finally,
\Ref{prop:unitary-adjoint-formulation}
identifies every selected unitary structure with a coherent
mate-symmetric equivalence
\[
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Base change}
\label{sec:equivariant-base-change}

Let
\[
    f
    \colon
    A
    \longrightarrow
    A'
\]
be a morphism in
\[
    \CAlg(\V).
\]
Write
\[
    F_f
    :=
    F_f^{\mathrm{rperf}}
    \colon
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}}
    \longrightarrow
    \Mor_{A'}^{\mathrm{rperf}}.
\]

No equivariance condition on the commutative base is required.

\begin{proposition}[Base change of Hermitian associative algebras]
\label{prop:base-change-hermitian-algebra}
Let
\[
    (B,\theta_B)
\]
be a Hermitian associative $A$-algebra.

The base-changed algebra
\[
    F_f(B)
    =
    B\otimes_AA'
\]
has a canonical Hermitian structure
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \theta_{F_f(B)}
    \colon
    F_f(B)
    &\xrightarrow{
        F_f(\theta_B)
    }
    F_f(B^{\op})
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \bigl(
            \omega_{f,B}^{\mathrm{alg}}
        \bigr)^{-1}
    }
    F_f(B)^{\op},
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
    \omega_{f,B}^{\mathrm{alg}}
    \colon
    F_f(B)^{\op}
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f(B^{\op})
\]
is the opposite-algebra comparison of
\Ref{prop:base-change-opposites}.

This construction defines a map
\[
    F_f^{\HermAlg}
    \colon
    \HermAlg_A
    \longrightarrow
    \HermAlg_{A'}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The composite
\[
\begin{aligned}
    F_f(B)
    &\xrightarrow{
        F_f(\theta_B)
    }
    F_f(B^{\op})
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \bigl(
            \omega_{f,B}^{\mathrm{alg}}
        \bigr)^{-1}
    }
    F_f(B)^{\op}
\end{aligned}
\]
is an equivalence of associative
\[
    A'
\]
-algebras.

The comparison
\[
    \omega_f
    \colon
    O_{A'}
    \circ
    \bigl(
        F_f^{\mathrm{Mor}}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f^{\mathrm{Mor}}
    \circ
    O_A
\]
intertwines the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures of the two opposite operations. It therefore sends the
fixed-point structure of
\[
    \theta_B
\]
to a fixed-point structure on
\[
    \theta_{F_f(B)}.
\]

Functoriality on Hermitian algebra equivalences gives
\[
    F_f^{\HermAlg}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Base change of transported hom duality]
\label{prop:base-change-transported-hom-duality}
Let
\[
    (B,\theta_B),
    \qquad
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be Hermitian associative $A$-algebras.

For every right-perfect
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \overline\kappa_{f,M}
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(M)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f
    \bigl(
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

These comparisons are coherently compatible with bimodule maps, Morita
composition, biduality, and the complete coherent
\[
    C_2
\]
-structures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The global base-change comparison of
\Ref{prop:base-change-morita-duality}
is
\[
    \kappa_f
    \colon
    \mathbb D_{A'}
    \circ
    F_f^{\mathrm{co}}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f
    \circ
    \mathbb D_A.
\]

By
\Ref{prop:base-change-hermitian-algebra},
the fixed-point structure on
\[
    F_f(B)
\]
is the transport of the fixed-point structure on
\[
    B
\]
through
\[
    (F_f,\kappa_f),
\]
and similarly for
\[
    C.
\]

Apply
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-functoriality}
with
\[
    \mathcal X
    =
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{rperf}},
    \qquad
    \mathcal Y
    =
    \Mor_{A'}^{\mathrm{rperf}},
\]
\[
    D
    =
    \mathbb D_A,
    \qquad
    E
    =
    \mathbb D_{A'},
    \qquad
    F
    =
    F_f.
\]
This gives
\[
    \overline\kappa_{f,M}
\]
and all the stated coherent compatibilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Base change of Hermitian reversal]
\label{prop:base-change-hermitian-reversal}
Let
\[
    (B,\theta_B),
    \qquad
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be Hermitian associative $A$-algebras. For every bimodule
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
\]
there is a canonical natural equivalence
\[
    j_{f,M}
    \colon
    J_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(M)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f
    \bigl(
        J_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr).
\]

For composable bimodules
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    C,
    \qquad
    N
    \colon
    C
    \longrightarrow
    D,
\]
the comparisons satisfy the canonically typed identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
    F_f(\zeta_{M,N})
    \circ
    j_{f,M\otimes_CN}
    \simeq{}&
    \phi_{f;J_{C,D}(N),J_{B,C}(M)}^{-1}
    \\
    &\circ
    \bigl(
        j_{f,N}
        \otimes_{F_f(C)}
        j_{f,M}
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\circ
    \zeta_{F_f(M),F_f(N)}
    \circ
    J_{F_f(B),F_f(D)}
    \bigl(
        \phi_{f;M,N}
    \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
    \phi_{f;M,N}
    \colon
    F_f(M\otimes_CN)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f(M)
    \otimes_{F_f(C)}
    F_f(N)
\]
is the Morita-composition comparison of
\Ref{prop:morita-base-change}.
They are also compatible with double Hermitian reversal:
\[
    F_f(\epsilon^J_M)
    \circ
    j^{(2)}_{f,M}
    \simeq
    \epsilon^J_{F_f(M)},
\]
where
\[
    j^{(2)}_{f,M}
\]
is the comparison obtained by applying
\[
    j_f
\]
twice. All identities hold with their complete higher coherences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The coherent action-reversal involutions and base change are related by
\[
    \omega_f
    \colon
    O_{A'}
    \circ
    \bigl(
        F_f^{\mathrm{Mor}}
    \bigr)^{\op}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f^{\mathrm{Mor}}
    \circ
    O_A.
\]
The Hermitian structures on
\[
    F_f(B)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    F_f(C)
\]
are precisely the fixed-point structures transported through
\[
    (F_f^{\mathrm{Mor}},\omega_f).
\]
Apply
\Ref{prop:fixed-endpoint-transport-one-op-functoriality}
with
\[
    \mathcal X
    =
    \Mor_A,
    \qquad
    \mathcal Y
    =
    \Mor_{A'},
\]
\[
    O_{\mathcal X}
    =
    O_A,
    \qquad
    O_{\mathcal Y}
    =
    O_{A'}.
\]
It gives the displayed comparison
\[
    j_{f,M}.
\]
Its compatibility with reversal of composition, double reversal, and all
higher involutivity data is part of the functorial fixed-endpoint
transport theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Base change of unitary bimodules]
\label{prop:base-change-unitary-bimodules}
Let
\[
    (M,s_M)
    \colon
    (B,\theta_B)
    \longrightarrow
    (C,\theta_C)
\]
be a unitary right-perfect bimodule.

Then
\[
    F_f(M)
    =
    M\otimes_AA'
\]
has a canonical unitary structure
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{F_f(M)}
    \colon
    F_f(M)
    &\xrightarrow{
        F_f(s_M)
    }
    F_f
    \bigl(
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \overline\kappa_{f,M}^{-1}
    }
    \mathbb D_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(M)
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the adjoint formulation, let
\[
    u_M
    \colon
    J_{B,C}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R
\]
be the mate-symmetric equivalence corresponding to
\[
    s_M.
\]
Then the base-changed mate-symmetric equivalence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    u_{F_f(M)}
    \colon
    J_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(M)
    \bigr)
    &\xrightarrow{
        j_{f,M}
    }
    F_f
    \bigl(
        J_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        F_f(u_M)
    }
    F_f(M^R)
    \\
    &\xrightarrow{
        \beta_{f,M}^{-1}
    }
    F_f(M)^R,
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
    j_{f,M}
    \colon
    J_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(M)
    \bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f
    \bigl(
        J_{B,C}(M)
    \bigr)
\]
is the Hermitian-reversal comparison and
\[
    \beta_{f,M}
    \colon
    F_f(M)^R
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f(M^R)
\]
is the right-adjoint comparison of
\Ref{prop:base-change-morita-adjoints}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Right perfectness is preserved by
\Ref{prop:base-change-morita-adjoints}.

The functor
\[
    F_f
\]
with comparison
\[
    \overline\kappa_f
\]
is a morphism of the transported coherent dualities. Hence it induces a
map of homotopy-fixed-point spaces. The image of
\[
    s_M
\]
under this map has underlying equivalence
\[
    s_{F_f(M)}.
\]
Its complete coherent fixed-point structure is therefore supplied
functorially.

The comparison
\[
    j_{f,M}
\]
is the coherent fixed-endpoint $1$-op transport comparison of
\Ref{prop:base-change-hermitian-reversal}.
The comparison
\[
    \beta_{f,M}^{-1}
\]
identifies the base change of the right adjoint with the right adjoint of
the base change.

Compatibility of
\[
    \beta_f
\]
with right mates and compatibility of
\[
    j_f
\]
with double Hermitian reversal transport the mate symmetry of
\[
    u_M
\]
to the mate symmetry of
\[
    u_{F_f(M)}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Base change of the global Hermitian Morita category]
\label{thm:base-change-hermitian-morita-category}
Extension of scalars induces an $(\infty,2)$-functor
\[
    F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}
    \colon
    \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}
    \longrightarrow
    \Mor_{A'}^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}}.
\]

It is compatible with the global dagger involutions through a coherent
natural equivalence
\[
    F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}
    \circ
    \dagger_A
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    \dagger_{A'}
    \circ
    \bigl(
        F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}
    \bigr)^{\mathrm{co}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By
\Ref{prop:base-change-hermitian-algebra},
extension of scalars induces
\[
    F_f^{\HermAlg}
    \colon
    \HermAlg_A
    \longrightarrow
    \HermAlg_{A'}.
\]

For every pair of Hermitian $A$-algebras
\[
    B,
    \qquad
    C,
\]
\Ref{prop:base-change-unitary-bimodules}
and functoriality of fixed-point decoration give
\[
\begin{aligned}
    F_{f;B,C}^{\mathrm{unit}}
    \colon
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{B,C}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H^{\mathrm{unit}}_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These functors are natural in Hermitian endpoint equivalences. They
therefore give a map of the unstraightened degree-one categories.

Extension of scalars preserves regular identity bimodules and Morita
composition through
\[
    F_f(M\otimes_CN)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_f(M)
    \otimes_{F_f(C)}
    F_f(N).
\]
Compatibility of
\[
    \overline\kappa_f
\]
with the Morita-duality multi-compositors shows that these comparisons
preserve the induced unitary structures.

Consequently, extension of scalars defines a morphism of Segal objects
\[
    \mathcal N_{A,\bullet}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal N_{A',\bullet}.
\]
Functoriality of completion gives
\[
    F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}.
\]

Compatibility with the dagger is the fixed-point-decoration
functoriality of
\Ref{prop:fixed-point-decoration-functoriality}.
Explicitly, for
\[
    \alpha
    \colon
    (M,s_M)
    \longrightarrow
    (N,s_N),
\]
naturality of
\[
    \overline\kappa_f
\]
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \overline\kappa_{f,M}
    \circ
    \mathbb D_{F_f(B),F_f(C)}
    \bigl(
        F_f(\alpha)
    \bigr)
    \simeq
    F_f
    \bigl(
        \mathbb D_{B,C}(\alpha)
    \bigr)
    \circ
    \overline\kappa_{f,N}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
\[
    s_{F_f(M)}
    =
    \overline\kappa_{f,M}^{-1}
    \circ
    F_f(s_M),
\]
one obtains
\[
    F_f(\alpha)^\dagger
    \simeq
    F_f(\alpha^\dagger).
\]

The complete compatibility with the involutivity modifications and
higher
\[
    C_2
\]
-coherences follows from the corresponding compatibility of
\[
    \kappa_f.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pseudofunctoriality in the commutative base]
\label{prop:hermitian-morita-base-change-pseudofunctorial}
For composable morphisms
\[
    A
    \xrightarrow{f}
    A'
    \xrightarrow{g}
    A'',
\]
the base-change compositor
\[
    \gamma_{g,f}
    \colon
    F_gF_f
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_{g\circ f}
\]
of
\Ref{prop:morita-base-change}
induces a pseudonatural adjoint equivalence
\[
    F_g^{\mathrm{Herm}}
    F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}
    \xRightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    F_{g\circ f}^{\mathrm{Herm}}.
\]

These comparisons satisfy the pseudofunctorial pentagon and unit
coherences and are compatible with the global dagger involutions.

Thus the assignment
\[
    A
    \longmapsto
    \bigl(
        \Mor_A^{\mathrm{Herm,rperf}},
        \dagger_A
    \bigr)
\]
is pseudofunctorial in
\[
    A\in\CAlg(\V).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pseudofunctorial comparison
\[
    \gamma_{g,f}
\]
is compatible with the global Morita dualities through the modification
of
\Ref{prop:base-change-morita-duality-composition}.

Applying fixed-endpoint transport sends this modification to the
corresponding comparison of transported hom dualities. Applying
homotopy fixed points and object decoration then gives the comparison on
unitary hom categories.

These comparisons preserve the coherent multi-composition and unit
functors. Hence they define a pseudonatural comparison between the
associated Segal objects and, after completion, between
\[
    F_g^{\mathrm{Herm}}F_f^{\mathrm{Herm}}
\]
and
\[
    F_{g\circ f}^{\mathrm{Herm}}.
\]

The pentagon and unit coherences are inherited from those of
\[
    \gamma.
\]
Compatibility with the dagger follows from the compatibility of the
modifications
\[
    \Theta_{g,f}
\]
with the complete coherent weak-duality structures.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples}
\label{sec:examples-relative-hermitian-morita}

\begin{example}[Associative algebras with coherent anti-involution]
\label{ex:derived-star-algebras}
Let
\[
    A
\]
be a commutative ring or an
\[
    \EE_\infty
\]
-ring.

A Hermitian associative $A$-algebra is an associative $A$-algebra
equipped with a coherent anti-involution
\[
    B
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B^{\op}.
\]

These include derived and spectral analogues of associative algebras with
involution. No conjugation on
\[
    A
\]
is used.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Endomorphism algebras]
\label{ex:hermitian-endomorphism-algebras}
Let
\[
    (M,h_M)
\]
be a Hermitian perfect $A$-module.

Then
\[
    \End_A(M)
\]
has the coherent anti-involution
\[
    f
    \longmapsto
    h_M^{-1}f^\vee h_M.
\]

Thus every Hermitian perfect module canonically determines a Hermitian
associative algebra.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Matrix algebra and the standard unitary Morita equivalence]
\label{ex:matrix-hermitian-morita}
Let
\[
    A
\]
be a commutative ring or an
\[
    \EE_\infty
\]
-ring, equipped with its canonical Hermitian structure
\[
    A
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    A^{\op}.
\]

Let
\[
    n\geq1
\]
and put
\[
    M
    :=
    A^{\oplus n}.
\]
Suppose
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^\vee
\]
is a Hermitian perfect structure. Define
\[
    B
    :=
    \End_A(M).
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item
    \[
        B
    \]
    is a Hermitian associative $A$-algebra with anti-involution
    \[
        b
        \longmapsto
        b^\dagger
        :=
        h_M^{-1}
        \circ
        b^\vee
        \circ
        h_M;
    \]

    \item the
    \[
        B\text{--}A
    \]
    -bimodule
    \[
        M
        \colon
        B
        \longrightarrow
        A
    \]
    is a Morita equivalence;

    \item the comparison
    \[
        h_M
        \colon
        J_{B,A}(M)
        \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
        M^R
    \]
    is mate-symmetric and defines a unitary structure on
    \[
        M.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
The Hermitian algebra structure on
\[
    B
    =
    \End_A(M)
\]
is supplied by
\Ref{prop:hermitian-endomorphism-algebra}.
Regard
\[
    M
\]
as a
\[
    B\text{--}A
\]
-bimodule, where
\[
    B
\]
acts on the left by evaluation. Since
\[
    M
    =
    A^{\oplus n}
\]
is perfect as an $A$-module, it is right perfect as a
\[
    B\text{--}A
\]
-bimodule, and
\[
    M^R
    \simeq
    M^\vee
\]
with its natural
\[
    A\text{--}B
\]
-bimodule structure.

Let
\[
    c_i
    \colon
    A
    \longrightarrow
    M,
    \qquad
    r_i
    \colon
    M
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
be the biproduct inclusions and projections. They satisfy
\[
    r_ic_j
    \simeq
    \delta_{ij}\id_A
\]
and
\[
    \sum_{i=1}^n c_ir_i
    \simeq
    \id_M.
\]
Define the matrix units
\[
    e_{ij}
    :=
    c_ir_j
    \in
    B.
\]
Then
\[
    e_{ij}e_{kl}
    \simeq
    \delta_{jk}e_{il}.
\]

Rigidity gives the rank-one-operator equivalence
\[
    \rho
    \colon
    M\otimes_AM^\vee
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    B,
\]
characterized by
\[
    \rho(m\otimes\phi)(x)
    =
    \phi(x)m.
\]
It is a
\[
    B\text{--}B
\]
-bimodule equivalence because
\[
    \rho(bm\otimes\phi)
    =
    b\circ\rho(m\otimes\phi)
\]
and
\[
    \rho(m\otimes\phi b)
    =
    \rho(m\otimes\phi)\circ b.
\]

The Morita unit
\[
    \eta
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    M\otimes_AM^\vee
\]
is explicitly
\[
    \eta(b)
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    bc_i\otimes r_i.
\]
Applying
\[
    \rho
\]
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \rho\eta(b)
    &=
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    bc_ir_i
    \\
    &=
    b.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
    \eta
    =
    \rho^{-1}
\]
is an equivalence.

Let
\[
    Q
    :=
    M^\vee\otimes_BM
\]
and let
\[
    q_0
    \colon
    M^\vee\otimes_AM
    \longrightarrow
    Q
\]
be the canonical balancing map. The Morita counit is evaluation
\[
    \epsilon
    \colon
    Q
    \longrightarrow
    A.
\]
Define
\[
    \sigma
    \colon
    A
    \longrightarrow
    Q
\]
by
\[
    \sigma
    :=
    q_0(r_1\otimes c_1).
\]
Then
\[
    \epsilon\sigma
    =
    r_1c_1
    =
    \id_A.
\]

Set
\[
    h
    :=
    \sigma\epsilon
    \colon
    Q
    \longrightarrow
    Q.
\]
For every matrix component,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    q_0(r_i\otimes c_j)
    &\simeq
    q_0(r_1e_{1i}\otimes c_j)
    \\
    &\simeq
    q_0(r_1\otimes e_{1i}c_j)
    \\
    &\simeq
    \delta_{ij}
    q_0(r_1\otimes c_1)
    \\
    &=
    \delta_{ij}\sigma.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h\,q_0(r_i\otimes c_j)
    &=
    \sigma\epsilon q_0(r_i\otimes c_j)
    \\
    &=
    \sigma(r_ic_j)
    \\
    &=
    \delta_{ij}\sigma.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
    h\circ q_0
    \simeq
    q_0.
\]
The maps
\[
    r_i\otimes c_j
    \colon
    A
    \longrightarrow
    M^\vee\otimes_AM
\]
are the inclusions of the finite biproduct decomposition of
\[
    M^\vee\otimes_AM.
\]
Thus the preceding equality holds on the entire degree-zero bar object.

Realize
\[
    Q
\]
as the two-sided bar construction
\[
    Q
    \simeq
    \left|
        [k]
        \longmapsto
        M^\vee
        \otimes_A
        B^{\otimes_A k}
        \otimes_A
        M
    \right|.
\]
Let
\[
    q_k
    \colon
    M^\vee
    \otimes_A
    B^{\otimes_A k}
    \otimes_A
    M
    \longrightarrow
    Q
\]
be its canonical cocone maps. Each
\[
    q_k
\]
factors as
\[
    q_k
    \simeq
    q_0\circ a_k,
\]
where
\[
    a_k
\]
is obtained by applying the iterated left and right
\[
    B
\]
-actions. Therefore
\[
    h\circ q_k
    \simeq
    h\circ q_0\circ a_k
    \simeq
    q_0\circ a_k
    \simeq
    q_k
\]
for every
\[
    k\geq0.
\]
Thus
\[
    h
\]
and
\[
    \id_Q
\]
agree on the complete canonical bar cocone. The universal property of
geometric realization gives
\[
    h
    \simeq
    \id_Q.
\]
Consequently,
\[
    \sigma\epsilon
    \simeq
    \id_Q,
    \qquad
    \epsilon\sigma
    \simeq
    \id_A.
\]
Hence
\[
    \epsilon
    \colon
    M^\vee\otimes_BM
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    A
\]
is an equivalence. Both the unit and counit of
\[
    M\dashv M^\vee
\]
are equivalences, so
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    A
\]
is a Morita equivalence.

The Hermitian reverse
\[
    J_{B,A}(M)
\]
is an
\[
    A\text{--}B
\]
-bimodule with underlying object
\[
    M.
\]
Its right
\[
    B
\]
-action is induced by the adjoint anti-involution:
\[
    m\cdot b
    :=
    b^\dagger m.
\]
The map
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    J_{B,A}(M)
    \longrightarrow
    M^\vee
\]
is left $A$-linear. It is right
\[
    B
\]
-linear because
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h_M(m\cdot b)
    &=
    h_M(b^\dagger m)
    \\
    &=
    h_M
    \bigl(
        h_M^{-1}b^\vee h_M(m)
    \bigr)
    \\
    &=
    b^\vee h_M(m)
    \\
    &=
    h_M(m)\cdot b.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    J_{B,A}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^\vee
    =
    M^R
\]
is an equivalence of
\[
    A\text{--}B
\]
-bimodules.

By
\Ref{prop:equivariant-endomorphism-morita-context},
the assignment
\[
    M
    \longmapsto
    \bigl(
        \End_A(M),
        M:\End_A(M)\to A
    \bigr)
\]
is coherently equivariant for perfect duality and canonical Morita
duality. Applying it to the complete Hermitian fixed point
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    M
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^\vee
\]
produces the complete unitary fixed-point structure on the Morita
morphism
\[
    M
    \colon
    B
    \longrightarrow
    A.
\]
Under the adjoint formulation of
\Ref{prop:unitary-adjoint-formulation},
its underlying mate-symmetric equivalence is precisely
\[
    h_M
    \colon
    J_{B,A}(M)
    \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}
    M^R.
\]
Therefore
\[
    M
\]
is a unitary Morita equivalence.
\end{proof}

Canonical Morita duality changes algebra endpoints to their opposites.
Hermitian endpoint structures transport this duality back to the
original hom categories. Unitary bimodules identify Hermitian reversal
with the Morita right adjoint, and conjugation by their selected
fixed-point equivalences equips every bimodule map with a coherent
dagger.
