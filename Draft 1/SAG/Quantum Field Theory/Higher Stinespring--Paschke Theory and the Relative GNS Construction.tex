\section{Aim and Derivational Policy}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-aim-policy}

This chapter develops a higher algebraic analogue of the
Stinespring--Paschke and GNS constructions inside the relative algebraic
geometry and Hermitian Morita theory established in the preceding chapter.

No independent ambient axiomatics are introduced here. The algebra objects,
module categories, dualities, Hermitian structures, adjoints, relative tensor
products, perfect objects, internal endomorphism objects, and unitary Morita
correspondences used below are exactly those already constructed and proved in
the preceding chapter.

Every notion introduced in this chapter is of one of the following forms.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It is a property of data already constructed in the preceding
  chapter.

  \item It is given by an explicit formula using previously constructed
  operations.

  \item It is characterized by a universal property whose existence is proved
  on the stated domain.

  \item It is an intrinsic restriction to the locus on which such a universal
  object exists.
\end{enumerate}

In particular, this chapter does not postulate a positive cone, an order
structure, a norm, a completion operation, a global dagger on the ambient
category, an image factorization, a radical quotient, or an abstract
``Stinespring category.''

When a canonical construction does not exist for every coefficient morphism,
the domain of the construction is defined intrinsically as the full locus on
which the required universal object exists. This restriction is a property of
the canonical realization category attached to the coefficient morphism. It
is not additional structure imposed on the ambient relative geometry.

All adjoints below are the local adjoints derived from Hermitian structures.
All pairings are those induced by unitary Morita duality. All coherence maps
are induced by previously constructed coherence or by universal properties,
and their higher coherence identities are proved using the contractibility of
the relevant mapping spaces.

The resulting theory therefore has the following logical form:
\[
  \boxed{
  \begin{gathered}
    \text{relative Hermitian Morita geometry}
    \\
    \Longrightarrow
    \\
    \text{matrix Hermitian-square calculus}
    \\
    \Longrightarrow
    \\
    \text{completely square-positive coefficient morphisms}
    \\
    \Longrightarrow
    \\
    \text{canonical Gram pre-correspondences}
    \\
    \Longrightarrow
    \\
    \text{higher Stinespring realizations on the admissible locus}
    \\
    \Longrightarrow
    \\
    \text{relative GNS theory.}
  \end{gathered}
  }
\]

\begin{convention}[Suppressed coherent data]
\label{conv:higher-stinespring-coherent-data}
Equivalences, adjunctions, Hermitian structures, fixed-point structures,
relative tensor products, and universal factorizations are understood with
their required coherent higher homotopies.

This convention suppresses notation only. It does not supply the existence of
any structure. Every such structure used below is either inherited from the
preceding chapter, explicitly constructed, or obtained from a stated universal
property.
\end{convention}

\section{Inherited Relative Hermitian Morita Geometry}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-inherited-setting}

Fix a presentably symmetric monoidal stable infinity-category
\[
  \mathcal V^\otimes
  \in
  \operatorname{CAlg}
  \bigl(
    \operatorname{Pr}^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{st}}
  \bigr)
\]
and a commutative algebra object
\[
  R\in\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathcal V).
\]

Write
\[
  \mathcal W
  :=
  \operatorname{Mod}_R(\mathcal V).
\]

The category \(\mathcal W\) is again presentably symmetric monoidal, stable,
and closed.

Throughout the chapter,
\[
  B,\ C,\ D,\ldots
\]
denote Hermitian associative algebra objects of \(\mathcal W\). Thus each such
algebra is equipped with a coherent equivalence
\[
  \sigma_B:
  B
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  B^{\mathrm{op}}
\]
satisfying the previously established homotopy-fixed-point condition.

After forgetting the multiplication and identifying the underlying
\(R\)-module of \(B^{\mathrm{op}}\) with that of \(B\), we also write
\[
  \sigma_B:B\xrightarrow{\simeq}B
\]
for the induced coherent involution on the underlying \(R\)-module.

It satisfies the anti-multiplicative identity
\[
  \sigma_B\mu_B
  \simeq
  \mu_B
  \tau
  (\sigma_B\otimes_R\sigma_B),
\]
where
\[
  \tau:B\otimes_RB\xrightarrow{\simeq}B\otimes_RB
\]
interchanges the two factors.

For Hermitian algebras \(B\) and \(C\), let
\[
  \mathcal H_R(B,C)
\]
denote the infinity-category of unitary right-perfect
\(B\)-\(C\)-bimodules constructed in the preceding chapter.

Thus an object
\[
  M\in\mathcal H_R(B,C)
\]
is a right-perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule equipped with a selected unitary
structure for the transported local duality.

Every such object carries a canonical nondegenerate \(C\)-valued Hermitian
pairing
\[
  \beta_M:
  M^\dagger\otimes_BM
  \longrightarrow
  C.
\]

For morphisms between unitary objects, write
\[
  f^\dagger
\]
for the previously constructed local adjoint.

Composition in the local Hermitian Morita theory is the relative tensor
product
\[
  -\otimes_C-:
  \mathcal H_R(B,C)
  \times
  \mathcal H_R(C,D)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_R(B,D).
\]

The compatibility of transported duality with Morita composition supplies
canonical equivalences
\[
  (M\otimes_CN)^\dagger
  \simeq
  N^\dagger\otimes_CM^\dagger
\]
and identifies the adjoint of a tensor product of morphisms with the tensor
product of their adjoints in reversed order.

\begin{proposition}[Inherited operations]
\label{prop:higher-stinespring-inherited-operations}
Every operation used in the remainder of this chapter is obtained from the
following previously constructed operations:
\[
  \oplus,
  \qquad
  \otimes_B,
  \qquad
  (-)^\dagger,
  \qquad
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_B,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{End}_B(-),
\]
together with algebra multiplication, module actions, finite limits, finite
colimits, and coherent equivalences already present in the relative
Hermitian Morita theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a dependency statement. Matrix objects are internal endomorphism
objects of finite biproducts. Hermitian squares are composites
\(T^\dagger T\). Coefficients are composites of a left action with an anchor
and its local adjoint. Gram forms use only the algebra multiplications,
involutions, the coefficient morphism, and the symmetry of \(\mathcal W\).
Stinespring comparison maps are induced by initiality in realization
infinity-categories. Composition uses relative tensor product. No additional
operation occurs.
\end{proof}

\section{Matrix Objects and Hermitian-Square Presentations}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-matrix-squares}

Let \(B\) be a Hermitian associative \(R\)-algebra.

For \(n\geq0\), define
\[
  B^{\oplus n}
\]
using the finite biproduct in the category of right \(B\)-modules, with
\[
  B^{\oplus0}:=0.
\]

The regular right \(B\)-module carries its canonical unitary structure, and
finite biproducts of unitary objects are unitary. Hence
\[
  B^{\oplus n}
\]
is a unitary perfect right \(B\)-module.

\begin{definition}[Matrix endomorphism object]
\label{def:higher-matrix-endomorphism-object}
Define
\[
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
  :=
  \operatorname{End}_B(B^{\oplus n}).
\]

Its multiplication is composition, and its Hermitian involution is the local
adjoint
\[
  X\longmapsto X^\dagger.
\]
\end{definition}

Let
\[
  \iota_j:B\to B^{\oplus n},
  \qquad
  \pi_i:B^{\oplus n}\to B
\]
be the biproduct injections and projections.

For
\[
  X\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(B),
\]
write
\[
  X_{ij}
  :=
  \pi_iX\iota_j
  \in B.
\]

The biproduct decomposition gives a canonical equivalence of underlying
\(R\)-modules
\[
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
  \simeq
  \bigoplus_{1\leq i,j\leq n}B.
\]

Under this equivalence,
\[
  X^\dagger
  =
  \bigl[\sigma_B(X_{ji})\bigr]_{i,j}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Hermitian-square presentation]
\label{def:higher-hermitian-square-presentation}
Let
\[
  X\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(B).
\]

A \textbf{Hermitian-square presentation} of \(X\) consists of:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a unitary perfect right \(B\)-module
  \[
    P\in\mathcal H_R(R,B);
  \]

  \item a right \(B\)-linear morphism
  \[
    T:B^{\oplus n}\to P;
  \]

  \item a specified equivalence
  \[
    X\simeq T^\dagger T.
  \]
\end{enumerate}

The endomorphism \(X\) is \textbf{Hermitian-square realizable} if the space of
such presentations is nonempty.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Witnessed positivity]
\label{rem:higher-witnessed-positivity}
Hermitian-square realizability is not an order relation and does not define a
positive cone.

The datum retained by the theory is the witness
\[
  T:B^{\oplus n}\to P
\]
and the factorization
\[
  X\simeq T^\dagger T.
\]

Allowing an arbitrary unitary perfect target \(P\) is essential. It ensures
that coefficients arising from arbitrary unitary perfect Morita
correspondences preserve square realizability without requiring the
correspondence to be finite free.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitian-square calculus]
\label{prop:higher-hermitian-square-calculus}
Let \(B\) be a Hermitian algebra.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every Hermitian-square realizable endomorphism is self-adjoint.

  \item The zero endomorphism is Hermitian-square realizable.

  \item Finite sums of Hermitian-square realizable endomorphisms are
  Hermitian-square realizable.

  \item If
  \[
    X\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
  \]
  is Hermitian-square realizable and
  \[
    A:B^{\oplus m}\to B^{\oplus n},
  \]
  then
  \[
    A^\dagger XA
    \in
    \operatorname{Mat}_m(B)
  \]
  is Hermitian-square realizable.

  \item If
  \[
    U:B^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{\simeq}B^{\oplus n}
  \]
  is unitary and \(X\) is Hermitian-square realizable, then
  \[
    UXU^\dagger
  \]
  is Hermitian-square realizable.

  \item Hermitian-square realizability is invariant under unitary equivalence
  of the target of a presentation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose
\[
  X\simeq T^\dagger T.
\]
Then
\[
  X^\dagger
  \simeq
  (T^\dagger T)^\dagger
  \simeq
  T^\dagger T
  \simeq
  X.
\]

The zero endomorphism has the presentation
\[
  B^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{0}0.
\]

If
\[
  X\simeq T^\dagger T,
  \qquad
  Y\simeq S^\dagger S,
\]
with
\[
  T:B^{\oplus n}\to P,
  \qquad
  S:B^{\oplus n}\to Q,
\]
then the biproduct morphism
\[
  \binom{T}{S}:
  B^{\oplus n}
  \longrightarrow
  P\oplus Q
\]
satisfies
\[
  \binom{T}{S}^{\dagger}
  \binom{T}{S}
  \simeq
  T^\dagger T+S^\dagger S
  \simeq
  X+Y.
\]

For compression,
\[
  A^\dagger XA
  \simeq
  A^\dagger T^\dagger TA
  \simeq
  (TA)^\dagger(TA).
\]

For unitary conjugation,
\[
  UXU^\dagger
  \simeq
  UT^\dagger TU^\dagger
  \simeq
  (TU^\dagger)^\dagger(TU^\dagger).
\]

Finally, if
\[
  V:P\xrightarrow{\simeq}Q
\]
is unitary, then
\[
  (VT)^\dagger(VT)
  \simeq
  T^\dagger V^\dagger VT
  \simeq
  T^\dagger T.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gram form of a finite family]
\label{prop:higher-gram-form-finite-family}
Let \(P\) be a unitary perfect right \(B\)-module and let
\[
  t_i:B\to P,
  \qquad
  i=1,\ldots,n,
\]
be right \(B\)-linear morphisms.

Then the matrix
\[
  G(t_1,\ldots,t_n)
  :=
  [t_i^\dagger t_j]_{i,j}
  \in
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
\]
is Hermitian-square realizable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
  T:B^{\oplus n}\to P
\]
be the unique morphism satisfying
\[
  T\iota_i=t_i.
\]

Then
\[
  \pi_iT^\dagger T\iota_j
  \simeq
  (T\iota_i)^\dagger(T\iota_j)
  \simeq
  t_i^\dagger t_j.
\]

Hence
\[
  T^\dagger T
  \simeq
  [t_i^\dagger t_j]_{i,j}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Hermitian Coefficient Morphisms}
\label{sec:higher-hermitian-coefficients}

Let \(B\) and \(C\) be Hermitian associative \(R\)-algebras.

The coherent involutions
\[
  \sigma_B:B\xrightarrow{\simeq}B,
  \qquad
  \sigma_C:C\xrightarrow{\simeq}C
\]
define an involution on the mapping space
\[
  \operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Mod}_R}(B,C)
\]
by
\[
  \Phi
  \longmapsto
  \Phi^\natural
  :=
  \sigma_C\Phi\sigma_B.
\]

\begin{definition}[Hermitian coefficient morphism]
\label{def:higher-hermitian-coefficient}
A \textbf{Hermitian coefficient morphism}
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
is a homotopy-fixed point for the preceding involution.

Equivalently, it is an \(R\)-linear morphism together with a coherent
equivalence
\[
  \Phi\sigma_B
  \simeq
  \sigma_C\Phi
\]
satisfying the corresponding \(C_2\)-coherence.

No multiplicativity or unitality is required.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Hermitianity is coherent data]
\label{rem:higher-hermitianity-coherent-data}
In an ordinary category, one may express Hermitianity by the equality
\[
  \Phi(b^\dagger)=\Phi(b)^\dagger.
\]

In the present higher setting, equality on homotopy classes does not by itself
supply the required fixed-point coherence. Hermitian coefficient morphisms
are therefore defined in the homotopy-fixed-point mapping space.
\end{remark}

For \(n\geq0\), the direct-sum decomposition gives an entrywise amplification
\[
  \Phi_n:
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(C),
\]
characterized by
\[
  \bigl(\Phi_n(X)\bigr)_{ij}
  =
  \Phi(X_{ij}).
\]

The Hermitian coherence of \(\Phi\) gives
\[
  \Phi_n(X^\dagger)
  \simeq
  \Phi_n(X)^\dagger.
\]

\begin{definition}[Completely square-positive coefficient]
\label{def:higher-completely-square-positive}
A Hermitian coefficient morphism
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
is \textbf{completely square-positive} if, for every \(n\geq0\),
\[
  X\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
  \quad\text{Hermitian-square realizable}
\]
implies
\[
  \Phi_n(X)
  \in
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(C)
  \quad\text{Hermitian-square realizable}.
\]

Write
\[
  \operatorname{CSP}_R(B,C)
\]
for the full subspace of the Hermitian coefficient mapping space spanned by
the completely square-positive coefficient morphisms.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Closure properties]
\label{prop:higher-csp-closure}
Completely square-positive coefficient morphisms satisfy the following
properties.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The identity morphism
  \[
    \operatorname{id}_B:B\to B
  \]
  is completely square-positive.

  \item If
  \[
    B\xrightarrow{\Phi}C\xrightarrow{\Psi}D
  \]
  are completely square-positive, then
  \[
    \Psi\Phi:B\to D
  \]
  is completely square-positive.

  \item If
  \[
    \Phi,\Psi:B\to C
  \]
  are completely square-positive, then
  \[
    \Phi+\Psi:B\to C
  \]
  is completely square-positive.

  \item Complete square positivity is invariant under equivalence of
  Hermitian coefficient morphisms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity preserves every square presentation.

For composition, if
\[
  X\simeq T^\dagger T,
\]
then complete square positivity of \(\Phi\) gives a presentation
\[
  \Phi_n(X)\simeq S^\dagger S.
\]
Applying complete square positivity of \(\Psi\) gives a square presentation of
\[
  \Psi_n\Phi_n(X)
  =
  (\Psi\Phi)_n(X).
\]

For sums, choose presentations
\[
  \Phi_n(X)\simeq T^\dagger T,
  \qquad
  \Psi_n(X)\simeq S^\dagger S.
\]
Then
\[
  (\Phi+\Psi)_n(X)
  \simeq
  T^\dagger T+S^\dagger S
\]
is square realizable by
\[
  \binom{T}{S}.
\]

The final assertion follows because square realizability is a property
invariant under equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-family Gram consequence]
\label{cor:higher-csp-finite-family-gram}
Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
be completely square-positive, and let
\[
  b_i:R\to B,
  \qquad
  i=1,\ldots,n,
\]
be generalized elements.

Then the matrix
\[
  \bigl[
    \Phi(\sigma_B(b_i)b_j)
  \bigr]_{i,j}
  \in
  \operatorname{Mat}_n(C)
\]
is Hermitian-square realizable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The row morphism determined by the \(b_i\) gives
\[
  T:B^{\oplus n}\to B.
\]
Its square is
\[
  T^\dagger T
  =
  [\sigma_B(b_i)b_j]_{i,j}.
\]
Apply \(\Phi_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No converse without generation hypotheses]
\label{rem:higher-no-gram-converse}
The finite-family Gram condition of
Corollary~\ref{cor:higher-csp-finite-family-gram} is a consequence of complete
square positivity.

No converse is asserted in the present generality. A converse would require
a theorem reducing arbitrary perfect square presentations to tests by global
elements or finite free targets. Such a reduction is not available from the
standing relative Hermitian Morita data alone.
\end{remark}

\section{Anchored Unitary Perfect Correspondences}
\label{sec:higher-anchored-correspondences}

Let
\[
  M\in\mathcal H_R(B,C)
\]
be a unitary right-perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule.

Its left action is represented by an algebra morphism
\[
  \lambda_M:
  B
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{End}_C(M).
\]

\begin{definition}[Anchor]
\label{def:higher-anchor}
An \textbf{anchor} on \(M\) is a right \(C\)-linear morphism
\[
  \xi:C\to M.
\]

Equivalently, by the free-forgetful adjunction, it is a generalized element
\[
  \overline\xi:R\to M.
\]
\end{definition}

Because \(C\) and \(M\) are unitary perfect right \(C\)-modules, the anchor has
a local adjoint
\[
  \xi^\dagger:M\to C.
\]

\begin{definition}[Coefficient of an anchored correspondence]
\label{def:higher-anchored-coefficient}
The coefficient of an anchored unitary perfect correspondence
\[
  (M,\xi):B\to C
\]
is the \(R\)-linear morphism
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}:B\to C
\]
defined by
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(b)
  :=
  \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(b)\xi.
\]

Equivalently,
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(b)
  =
  \langle\xi,b\xi\rangle_C.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitianity of anchored coefficients]
\label{prop:higher-anchored-coefficient-hermitian}
The coefficient
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}:B\to C
\]
is a Hermitian coefficient morphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The left action is compatible with the Hermitian structures:
\[
  \lambda_M(\sigma_B(b))
  \simeq
  \lambda_M(b)^\dagger.
\]

Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(\sigma_B(b))
  &=
  \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(\sigma_B(b))\xi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(b)^\dagger\xi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C
  \bigl(
    \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(b)\xi
  \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The coherence is inherited from the coherent Hermitian structures on \(B\),
\(C\), and \(M\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anchored coefficients are completely square-positive]
\label{thm:higher-anchored-coefficients-csp}
Let
\[
  (M,\xi):B\to C
\]
be an anchored unitary right-perfect correspondence.

Then
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}:B\to C
\]
is completely square-positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
  X\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(B)
\]
have a Hermitian-square presentation
\[
  X\simeq T^\dagger T
\]
with
\[
  T:B^{\oplus n}\to P
\]
and \(P\) a unitary perfect right \(B\)-module.

Morita composition gives a unitary perfect right \(C\)-module
\[
  P\otimes_BM.
\]

The anchor determines
\[
  \xi^{\oplus n}:
  C^{\oplus n}
  \longrightarrow
  M^{\oplus n}.
\]

Using the canonical equivalence
\[
  B^{\oplus n}\otimes_BM
  \simeq
  M^{\oplus n},
\]
define
\[
  \widetilde T:
  C^{\oplus n}
  \xrightarrow{\xi^{\oplus n}}
  M^{\oplus n}
  \xrightarrow{T\otimes_B\operatorname{id}_M}
  P\otimes_BM.
\]

Compatibility of transported adjoints with Morita composition gives
\[
  (T\otimes_B\operatorname{id}_M)^\dagger
  \simeq
  T^\dagger\otimes_B\operatorname{id}_M.
\]

Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde T^\dagger\widetilde T
  &\simeq
  (\xi^\dagger)^{\oplus n}
  (T^\dagger T\otimes_B\operatorname{id}_M)
  \xi^{\oplus n}
  \\
  &\simeq
  (\xi^\dagger)^{\oplus n}
  (X\otimes_B\operatorname{id}_M)
  \xi^{\oplus n}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the matrix-entry identification, the \((i,j)\)-entry of the last
endomorphism is
\[
  \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(X_{ij})\xi
  =
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(X_{ij}).
\]

Therefore
\[
  \widetilde T^\dagger\widetilde T
  \simeq
  (\Phi_{M,\xi})_n(X).
\]

Thus \((\Phi_{M,\xi})_n(X)\) is Hermitian-square realizable.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Anchored isometry and anchored unitary]
\label{def:higher-anchored-isometry}
Let
\[
  (M,\xi),
  \qquad
  (N,\zeta)
\]
be anchored unitary perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-correspondences.

An \textbf{anchored isometry}
\[
  U:(M,\xi)\to(N,\zeta)
\]
is a \(B\)-\(C\)-linear morphism
\[
  U:M\to N
\]
such that
\[
  U^\dagger U
  \simeq
  \operatorname{id}_M
\]
and
\[
  U\xi
  \simeq
  \zeta.
\]

It is an \textbf{anchored unitary} if \(U\) is an equivalence, equivalently if
\[
  U^\dagger
  \simeq
  U^{-1}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Anchored isometries preserve coefficients]
\label{prop:higher-anchored-isometries-preserve-coefficients}
An anchored isometry
\[
  U:(M,\xi)\to(N,\zeta)
\]
induces a coherent equivalence
\[
  \Phi_{M,\xi}
  \simeq
  \Phi_{N,\zeta}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(U\) is \(B\)-linear,
\[
  U\lambda_M(b)
  \simeq
  \lambda_N(b)U.
\]

Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_{N,\zeta}(b)
  &=
  \zeta^\dagger\lambda_N(b)\zeta
  \\
  &\simeq
  \xi^\dagger
  U^\dagger
  \lambda_N(b)
  U
  \xi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \xi^\dagger
  U^\dagger U
  \lambda_M(b)
  \xi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \xi^\dagger\lambda_M(b)\xi
  \\
  &=
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(b).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The Canonical Free Orbit Bimodule}
\label{sec:higher-free-orbit-bimodule}

Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
be a Hermitian coefficient morphism.

\begin{definition}[Free orbit bimodule]
\label{def:higher-free-orbit-bimodule}
Define
\[
  F_\Phi
  :=
  B\otimes_RC.
\]

It is a \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule using the regular left action of \(B\) on the
first factor and the regular right action of \(C\) on the second factor.

The \textbf{free anchor}
\[
  \xi_\Phi^0:C\to F_\Phi
\]
is the right \(C\)-linear morphism
\[
  C
  \simeq
  R\otimes_RC
  \xrightarrow{\eta_B\otimes_R\operatorname{id}_C}
  B\otimes_RC.
\]
\end{definition}

No perfectness or nondegeneracy assertion is made about \(F_\Phi\).

\subsection{The canonical Gram form}

Let
\[
  \tau:
  B\otimes_RC\otimes_RB\otimes_RC
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  C\otimes_RB\otimes_RB\otimes_RC
\]
be the symmetry permutation carrying
\[
  b\otimes c\otimes b'\otimes c'
\]
to
\[
  c\otimes b\otimes b'\otimes c'.
\]

\begin{definition}[Canonical Gram form]
\label{def:higher-canonical-gram-form}
The \textbf{canonical Gram form} associated to \(\Phi\) is the composite
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
  F_\Phi\otimes_RF_\Phi
  \arrow[r,
    "{\sigma_B\otimes\sigma_C\otimes
    \operatorname{id}_B\otimes\operatorname{id}_C}"]
  &
  B\otimes_RC\otimes_RB\otimes_RC
  \arrow[r,"\tau"]
  &
  C\otimes_RB\otimes_RB\otimes_RC
  \arrow[r,
    "{\operatorname{id}_C\otimes\mu_B\otimes\operatorname{id}_C}"]
  &
  C\otimes_RB\otimes_RC
  \arrow[r,
    "{\operatorname{id}_C\otimes\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_C}"]
  &
  C\otimes_RC\otimes_RC
  \arrow[r,"\mu_C^{(3)}"]
  &
  C.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

It is denoted
\[
  \Gamma_\Phi:
  F_\Phi\otimes_RF_\Phi
  \longrightarrow
  C.
\]
\end{definition}

In elementwise shorthand,
\[
  \Gamma_\Phi
  (b\otimes c,b'\otimes c')
  =
  \sigma_C(c)
  \Phi\bigl(\sigma_B(b)b'\bigr)
  c'.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Properties of the Gram form]
\label{prop:higher-gram-form-properties}
The canonical Gram form satisfies the following identities.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It is right \(C\)-linear in the second variable:
  \[
    \Gamma_\Phi(x,yc)
    \simeq
    \Gamma_\Phi(x,y)c.
  \]

  \item It is conjugate \(C\)-linear in the first variable:
  \[
    \Gamma_\Phi(xc,y)
    \simeq
    \sigma_C(c)\Gamma_\Phi(x,y).
  \]

  \item It is Hermitian:
  \[
    \Gamma_\Phi(y,x)
    \simeq
    \sigma_C\Gamma_\Phi(x,y).
  \]

  \item The left \(B\)-action is formally adjointable:
  \[
    \Gamma_\Phi(bx,y)
    \simeq
    \Gamma_\Phi(x,\sigma_B(b)y).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two identities follow directly from associativity of \(\mu_C\) and
anti-multiplicativity of \(\sigma_C\).

For Hermitian symmetry, compute on pure tensors:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \sigma_C
  \Gamma_\Phi
  (b\otimes c,b'\otimes c')
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C
  \left(
    \sigma_C(c)
    \Phi(\sigma_B(b)b')
    c'
  \right)
  \\
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C(c')
  \sigma_C\Phi(\sigma_B(b)b')
  c
  \\
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C(c')
  \Phi\bigl(
    \sigma_B(\sigma_B(b)b')
  \bigr)
  c
  \\
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C(c')
  \Phi(\sigma_B(b')b)
  c
  \\
  &=
  \Gamma_\Phi
  (b'\otimes c',b\otimes c).
\end{aligned}
\]

The fourth identity follows from
\[
  \sigma_B(bb_0)
  \simeq
  \sigma_B(b_0)\sigma_B(b).
\]
All identities are compatible with the higher coherences because they are
composites of the coherent algebra and fixed-point data.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The free comparison}

Let
\[
  (M,\xi):B\to C
\]
be an anchored unitary perfect correspondence.

The left action and the anchor define a \(B\)-\(C\)-linear morphism
\[
  q_{M,\xi}:F_{\Phi_{M,\xi}}\to M
\]
by the composite
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  B\otimes_RC
  \arrow[r,
    "{\operatorname{id}_B\otimes_R\xi}"]
  &
  B\otimes_RM
  \arrow[r,"\lambda_M"]
  &
  M.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

In elementwise shorthand,
\[
  q_{M,\xi}(b\otimes c)
  =
  b\xi c.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Free comparison preserves the Gram form]
\label{prop:higher-free-comparison-preserves-gram}
For every anchored unitary perfect correspondence \((M,\xi)\),
\[
  \beta_M
  \bigl(
    q_{M,\xi}(-),
    q_{M,\xi}(-)
  \bigr)
  \simeq
  \Gamma_{\Phi_{M,\xi}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On pure tensors,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \left\langle
    b\xi c,
    b'\xi c'
  \right\rangle_C
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C(c)
  \left\langle
    b\xi,
    b'\xi
  \right\rangle_C
  c'
  \\
  &\simeq
  \sigma_C(c)
  \left\langle
    \xi,
    \sigma_B(b)b'\xi
  \right\rangle_C
  c'
  \\
  &=
  \sigma_C(c)
  \Phi_{M,\xi}
  \bigl(
    \sigma_B(b)b'
  \bigr)
  c'
  \\
  &=
  \Gamma_{\Phi_{M,\xi}}
  (b\otimes c,b'\otimes c').
\end{aligned}
\]

The internal equality follows from the universal property of
\(B\otimes_RC\).
\end{proof}

\section{Realization Infinity-Categories}
\label{sec:higher-realization-categories}

Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
be a completely square-positive Hermitian coefficient morphism.

\begin{definition}[Perfect Stinespring realization]
\label{def:higher-perfect-stinespring-realization}
A \textbf{perfect Stinespring realization} of \(\Phi\) consists of:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a unitary right-perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule
  \[
    M\in\mathcal H_R(B,C);
  \]

  \item an anchor
  \[
    \xi:C\to M;
  \]

  \item a coherent equivalence of Hermitian coefficient morphisms
  \[
    \alpha:
    \Phi_{M,\xi}
    \xrightarrow{\simeq}
    \Phi.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Realization infinity-category]
\label{def:higher-realization-category}
Define
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
\]
to be the infinity-category whose objects are perfect Stinespring
realizations
\[
  (M,\xi,\alpha)
\]
of \(\Phi\).

A morphism
\[
  (M,\xi,\alpha)
  \longrightarrow
  (N,\zeta,\beta)
\]
is an anchored isometry
\[
  U:(M,\xi)\to(N,\zeta)
\]
whose induced coefficient equivalence is compatible with \(\alpha\) and
\(\beta\).
\end{definition}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-anchored-isometries-preserve-coefficients},
the coefficient compatibility is a coherent condition on the already
constructed anchored isometry.

\subsection{Gram factorizations}

\begin{definition}[Perfect Gram factorization]
\label{def:higher-perfect-gram-factorization}
A \textbf{perfect Gram factorization} of \(\Phi\) consists of:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a unitary right-perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule \(M\);

  \item a \(B\)-\(C\)-linear morphism
  \[
    q:F_\Phi\to M;
  \]

  \item a coherent equivalence
  \[
    \beta_M(q\otimes q)
    \xrightarrow{\simeq}
    \Gamma_\Phi.
  \]
\end{enumerate}

Morphisms of perfect Gram factorizations are isometries commuting with the
maps from \(F_\Phi\) and preserving the displayed equivalences.

Write
\[
  \operatorname{GramFact}_R(\Phi)
\]
for the resulting infinity-category.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Realizations and Gram factorizations]
\label{prop:higher-realizations-gram-factorizations}
There is a canonical equivalence
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{GramFact}_R(\Phi).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given a realization
\[
  (M,\xi,\alpha),
\]
the free comparison
\[
  q_{M,\xi}:F_\Phi\to M
\]
is obtained from the free comparison for \(\Phi_{M,\xi}\) by transport along
\(\alpha\). Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-free-comparison-preserves-gram}
shows that it preserves the Gram form.

Conversely, let
\[
  (M,q,\varepsilon)
\]
be a perfect Gram factorization. Define the anchor
\[
  \xi:
  C
  \xrightarrow{\xi_\Phi^0}
  F_\Phi
  \xrightarrow{q}
  M.
\]

For \(b\in B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_{M,\xi}(b)
  &=
  \langle\xi,b\xi\rangle_C
  \\
  &\simeq
  \Gamma_\Phi
  \bigl(
    1_B\otimes1_C,
    b\otimes1_C
  \bigr)
  \\
  &=
  \Phi(b).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \((M,\xi)\) is a realization of \(\Phi\).

The two constructions are inverse because a \(B\)-\(C\)-linear map out of
\(F_\Phi=B\otimes_RC\) is determined by the image of the free anchor.
Compatibility on morphisms follows from the same universal property.
\end{proof}

\section{The Intrinsic Admissible Locus}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-admissible-locus}

The preceding constructions exist for every completely square-positive
coefficient morphism. A distinguished minimal realization need not exist for
every such morphism.

\begin{definition}[Perfect Stinespring admissibility]
\label{def:higher-stinespring-admissible}
A completely square-positive Hermitian coefficient morphism
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
is \textbf{perfectly Stinespring-admissible} if the infinity-category
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
\]
has an initial object.

Equivalently, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-realizations-gram-factorizations},
\(\Phi\) is admissible if
\[
  \operatorname{GramFact}_R(\Phi)
\]
has an initial object.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Admissibility is a property]
\label{rem:higher-admissibility-property}
Admissibility is not additional structure on \(B\), \(C\), or the ambient
relative geometry.

It is the intrinsic property that the realization infinity-category
canonically attached to \(\Phi\) possesses an initial object.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No unrestricted existence claim]
\label{rem:higher-no-unrestricted-stinespring}
No claim is made that every completely square-positive coefficient morphism
is admissible.

Such a claim would require a general method for producing a nondegenerate
perfect support from the possibly degenerate Gram form
\[
  \Gamma_\Phi
\]
on
\[
  F_\Phi=B\otimes_RC.
\]

A bare stable infinity-category does not supply a canonical image object or
radical quotient with the required universal property.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Contractibility of minimal choices]
\label{prop:higher-contractibility-minimal-choices}
If \(\Phi\) is perfectly Stinespring-admissible, then the space of initial
objects of
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
\]
is contractible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard contractibility of the space of initial objects in an
infinity-category.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Higher Stinespring correspondence]
\label{def:higher-stinespring-correspondence}
Let \(\Phi\) be perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

Choose an initial object
\[
  (S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi,\alpha_\Phi)
  \in
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi).
\]

The object
\[
  S_\Phi
\]
is the \textbf{higher Stinespring--Paschke correspondence} of \(\Phi\), and
\[
  \xi_\Phi:C\to S_\Phi
\]
is its \textbf{minimal anchor}.
\end{definition}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-contractibility-minimal-choices}, this choice
is canonical up to a contractible space of choices.

\section{The Higher Stinespring Realization Theorem}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-realization}

\begin{theorem}[Higher Stinespring--Paschke realization]
\label{thm:higher-stinespring-realization}
Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
be a perfectly Stinespring-admissible completely square-positive Hermitian
coefficient morphism.

Then there exist:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a unitary right-perfect \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule
  \[
    S_\Phi;
  \]

  \item an anchor
  \[
    \xi_\Phi:C\to S_\Phi;
  \]

  \item a coherent coefficient equivalence
  \[
    \Phi(b)
    \simeq
    \langle\xi_\Phi,b\xi_\Phi\rangle_C.
  \]
\end{enumerate}

Moreover, for every perfect realization
\[
  (M,\xi,\alpha)
  \in
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi),
\]
the mapping space
\[
  \operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)}
  \bigl(
    (S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi),
    (M,\xi)
  \bigr)
\]
is contractible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The existence of \((S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi)\) is precisely the initial-object
condition in
Definition~\ref{def:higher-stinespring-admissible}.

The coefficient identity is part of the realization datum. Equivalently, it
follows from the Gram-factorization description:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \langle\xi_\Phi,b\xi_\Phi\rangle_C
  &\simeq
  \Gamma_\Phi
  \bigl(
    1_B\otimes1_C,
    b\otimes1_C
  \bigr)
  \\
  &=
  \Phi(b).
\end{aligned}
\]

The contractibility statement is the defining mapping-space property of an
initial object.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Minimal unitary uniqueness]
\label{cor:higher-minimal-unitary-uniqueness}
Let
\[
  (M,\xi),
  \qquad
  (N,\zeta)
\]
be initial objects of
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi).
\]

Then the space of anchored unitary equivalences
\[
  (M,\xi)
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  (N,\zeta)
\]
is contractible.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There are contractible spaces of maps in both directions. Their composites
are endomorphisms of initial objects and therefore lie in contractible mapping
spaces containing the identities. Hence the maps are mutually inverse
equivalences.

Since every morphism in the realization category is an isometry, an
equivalence is unitary.
\end{proof}

\section{The Internal Stinespring Representation}
\label{sec:higher-internal-stinespring-representation}

Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C
\]
be perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

The left \(B\)-action on \(S_\Phi\) is represented by an algebra morphism
\[
  \pi_\Phi:
  B
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{End}_C(S_\Phi).
\]

Set
\[
  V_\Phi:=\xi_\Phi:C\to S_\Phi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Internal Stinespring formula]
\label{thm:higher-internal-stinespring-formula}
The triple
\[
  (S_\Phi,\pi_\Phi,V_\Phi)
\]
satisfies
\[
  \Phi(b)
  \simeq
  V_\Phi^\dagger
  \pi_\Phi(b)
  V_\Phi.
\]

The morphism
\[
  \pi_\Phi:
  B\to\operatorname{End}_C(S_\Phi)
\]
is unital and multiplicative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formula is the coefficient recovery formula:
\[
  \Phi(b)
  \simeq
  \xi_\Phi^\dagger\lambda_{S_\Phi}(b)\xi_\Phi.
\]

The morphism \(\pi_\Phi\) represents the left \(B\)-module action. The
associativity and unitality of that action imply the multiplicativity and
unitality of \(\pi_\Phi\) by the representing property of
\(\operatorname{End}_C(S_\Phi)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitian compatibility of the representation]
\label{prop:higher-stinespring-representation-hermitian}
The Stinespring representation satisfies
\[
  \pi_\Phi(\sigma_B(b))
  \simeq
  \pi_\Phi(b)^\dagger.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the compatibility of the left \(B\)-action with the unitary structure
on the \(B\)-\(C\)-bimodule \(S_\Phi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Normalized coefficients and isometric anchors]
\label{prop:higher-normalized-anchor-isometry}
Suppose
\[
  \Phi(\eta_B)
  \simeq
  \eta_C.
\]

Then
\[
  V_\Phi^\dagger V_\Phi
  \simeq
  \operatorname{id}_C.
\]

Thus the minimal anchor is an isometry.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\pi_\Phi\) is unital,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_\Phi^\dagger V_\Phi
  &\simeq
  V_\Phi^\dagger
  \pi_\Phi(\eta_B)
  V_\Phi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \Phi(\eta_B)
  \\
  &\simeq
  \eta_C.
\end{aligned}
\]
The unit \(\eta_C\) acts as the identity endomorphism of the regular right
\(C\)-module.
\end{proof}

\section{Dilations, Minimality, and Support Projections}
\label{sec:higher-dilations-minimality}

Let \(\Phi\) be perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

For every realization
\[
  (M,\xi,\alpha)
  \in
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi),
\]
initiality gives a contractible space of anchored isometries
\[
  j_{M,\xi}:
  S_\Phi\to M.
\]

\begin{definition}[Perfect Stinespring dilation]
\label{def:higher-perfect-stinespring-dilation}
A \textbf{perfect Stinespring dilation} of \(\Phi\) is an object of
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi).
\]

The canonical anchored isometry
\[
  j_{M,\xi}:S_\Phi\to M
\]
is its \textbf{minimal comparison}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Support projection]
\label{def:higher-support-projection}
The \textbf{support projection} of a perfect dilation \((M,\xi)\) is
\[
  p_{M,\xi}
  :=
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  \in
  \operatorname{End}_{B\text{-}C}(M).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Support projection calculus]
\label{prop:higher-support-projection-calculus}
The support projection satisfies
\[
  p_{M,\xi}^2
  \simeq
  p_{M,\xi},
  \qquad
  p_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  \simeq
  p_{M,\xi}.
\]

Moreover,
\[
  p_{M,\xi}\xi
  \simeq
  \xi.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(j_{M,\xi}\) is an isometry,
\[
  j_{M,\xi}^\dagger j_{M,\xi}
  \simeq
  \operatorname{id}_{S_\Phi}.
\]

Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  p_{M,\xi}^2
  &=
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  \\
  &\simeq
  j_{M,\xi}
  \operatorname{id}_{S_\Phi}
  j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  \\
  &=
  p_{M,\xi}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Also,
\[
  p_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  \simeq
  (j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger)^\dagger
  \simeq
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger.
\]

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  p_{M,\xi}\xi
  &\simeq
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  j_{M,\xi}\xi_\Phi
  \\
  &\simeq
  j_{M,\xi}\xi_\Phi
  \\
  &\simeq
  \xi.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

The preceding chapter proves that Hermitian idempotents split in the perfect
Hermitian setting. Therefore the support projection determines a unitary
retract of \(M\).

\begin{proposition}[The minimal correspondence as a Hermitian retract]
\label{prop:higher-minimal-as-hermitian-retract}
The Hermitian idempotent
\[
  p_{M,\xi}
\]
splits, and its split image is canonically equivalent to
\[
  S_\Phi.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The splitting is supplied by the Hermitian idempotent-completeness theorem.

The maps
\[
  j_{M,\xi}:S_\Phi\to M,
  \qquad
  j_{M,\xi}^\dagger:M\to S_\Phi
\]
satisfy
\[
  j_{M,\xi}^\dagger j_{M,\xi}
  \simeq
  \operatorname{id}_{S_\Phi}
\]
and
\[
  j_{M,\xi}j_{M,\xi}^\dagger
  =
  p_{M,\xi}.
\]

Thus they already exhibit \(S_\Phi\) as a splitting of \(p_{M,\xi}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Minimal realization]
\label{def:higher-minimal-realization}
A perfect realization
\[
  (M,\xi)
  \in
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
\]
is \textbf{minimal} if its minimal comparison
\[
  j_{M,\xi}:S_\Phi\to M
\]
is an equivalence.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Equivalent minimality criteria]
\label{prop:higher-equivalent-minimality-criteria}
For a perfect realization \((M,\xi)\), the following are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \((M,\xi)\) is minimal.

  \item The support projection satisfies
  \[
    p_{M,\xi}
    \simeq
    \operatorname{id}_M.
  \]

  \item \((M,\xi)\) is an initial object of
  \[
    \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi).
  \]

  \item \((M,\xi)\) is anchored unitarily equivalent to
  \[
    (S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An isometry is an equivalence precisely when its range projection is the
identity. This proves the equivalence of the first two conditions.

The initial objects of an infinity-category form a contractible space, proving
the equivalence of the first, third, and fourth conditions.
\end{proof}

\section{Homotopies of Coefficients and Higher Naturality}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-homotopy-naturality}

Let
\[
  \Phi,\Psi:B\to C
\]
be completely square-positive Hermitian coefficient morphisms.

A coherent path
\[
  \eta:\Phi\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Psi
\]
induces an equivalence
\[
  \eta_*:
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Psi)
\]
by composing each coefficient identification with \(\eta\).

\begin{proposition}[Invariance of admissibility]
\label{prop:higher-admissibility-homotopy-invariant}
If
\[
  \Phi\simeq\Psi
\]
and \(\Phi\) is perfectly Stinespring-admissible, then \(\Psi\) is perfectly
Stinespring-admissible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equivalent infinity-categories have initial objects simultaneously.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Higher naturality of minimal realizations]
\label{thm:higher-stinespring-homotopy-naturality}
Let
\[
  \eta:\Phi\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Psi
\]
be an equivalence between perfectly Stinespring-admissible coefficients.

Then there is a canonical anchored unitary equivalence
\[
  S_\Phi
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  S_\Psi
\]
compatible with \(\eta\).

The space of such equivalences is contractible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence
\[
  \eta_*:
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi)
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Psi)
\]
carries initial objects to initial objects. The result follows from the
contractibility of the space of initial objects and of the mapping space
between them.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Higher coherence]
\label{cor:higher-stinespring-higher-coherence}
Identity paths induce identity equivalences, concatenation of paths induces
composition of Stinespring equivalences, and higher homotopies induce the
corresponding higher homotopies between these equivalences.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
All choices lie in contractible spaces determined by initiality.
\end{proof}

\section{Composition of Anchored Realizations}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-composition}

Let
\[
  (M,\xi):B\to C,
  \qquad
  (N,\zeta):C\to D
\]
be anchored unitary right-perfect correspondences.

Let
\[
  \overline\xi:R\to M,
  \qquad
  \overline\zeta:R\to N
\]
be the underlying generalized elements.

The symmetry and the quotient map to the relative tensor product define
\[
  R
  \simeq
  R\otimes_RR
  \xrightarrow{\overline\xi\otimes_R\overline\zeta}
  M\otimes_RN
  \longrightarrow
  M\otimes_CN.
\]

By the free right \(D\)-module adjunction, this generalized element determines
a right \(D\)-linear anchor
\[
  \xi\boxtimes\zeta:
  D\to M\otimes_CN.
\]

\begin{definition}[Tensor product of anchored correspondences]
\label{def:higher-anchored-tensor-product}
Define
\[
  (M,\xi)\boxtimes_C(N,\zeta)
  :=
  \bigl(
    M\otimes_CN,
    \xi\boxtimes\zeta
  \bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient composition]
\label{prop:higher-coefficient-composition}
The coefficient of the anchored tensor product is
\[
  \Phi_{(M,\xi)\boxtimes_C(N,\zeta)}
  \simeq
  \Phi_{N,\zeta}
  \circ
  \Phi_{M,\xi}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(b\in B\), the tensor-product pairing gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \left\langle
    \xi\boxtimes\zeta,
    b(\xi\boxtimes\zeta)
  \right\rangle_D
  &\simeq
  \left\langle
    \zeta,
    \left\langle
      \xi,
      b\xi
    \right\rangle_C
    \zeta
  \right\rangle_D
  \\
  &=
  \Phi_{N,\zeta}
  \bigl(
    \Phi_{M,\xi}(b)
  \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Tensoring realizations]
\label{cor:higher-tensoring-realizations}
Let
\[
  \Phi:B\to C,
  \qquad
  \Psi:C\to D
\]
be completely square-positive coefficients.

If
\[
  (M,\xi)\in\operatorname{Real}_R(\Phi),
  \qquad
  (N,\zeta)\in\operatorname{Real}_R(\Psi),
\]
then
\[
  (M,\xi)\boxtimes_C(N,\zeta)
\]
is an object of
\[
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\Psi\Phi).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-coefficient-composition}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Canonical Colax Stinespring Comparison}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-colax-comparison}

Let
\[
  B\xrightarrow{\Phi}C\xrightarrow{\Psi}D
\]
be perfectly Stinespring-admissible, and assume that the composite
\[
  \Psi\Phi:B\to D
\]
is also perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

The anchored tensor product
\[
  (S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi)
  \boxtimes_C
  (S_\Psi,\xi_\Psi)
\]
is a realization of \(\Psi\Phi\).

Initiality therefore gives a contractible space of anchored isometries
\[
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}:
  S_{\Psi\Phi}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Canonical colax comparison]
\label{thm:higher-stinespring-colax-comparison}
The comparison
\[
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}:
  S_{\Psi\Phi}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi
\]
is an anchored isometry.

Its support projection
\[
  p_{\Phi,\Psi}
  :=
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}^\dagger
\]
is a Hermitian idempotent on
\[
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi.
\]

The split image of \(p_{\Phi,\Psi}\) is canonically equivalent to
\[
  S_{\Psi\Phi}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The comparison is the unique morphism from the initial object
\[
  S_{\Psi\Phi}
\]
to the tensor-product realization. Morphisms in the realization category are
anchored isometries.

The projection statement follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-support-projection-calculus}, and its split image
is identified by
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-minimal-as-hermitian-retract}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Perfect cyclic tensor support]
\label{def:higher-perfect-cyclic-tensor-support}
The \textbf{perfect cyclic tensor support} of
\[
  S_\Phi
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  S_\Psi
\]
is the Hermitian retract of
\[
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi
\]
cut out by
\[
  p_{\Phi,\Psi}.
\]

It is denoted
\[
  S_\Phi
  \boxtimes_C^{\mathrm{cyc}}
  S_\Psi.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Minimal composite support]
\label{cor:higher-minimal-composite-support}
There is a canonical anchored unitary equivalence
\[
  S_{\Psi\Phi}
  \xrightarrow{\simeq}
  S_\Phi
  \boxtimes_C^{\mathrm{cyc}}
  S_\Psi.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both objects split the same Hermitian idempotent
\[
  p_{\Phi,\Psi}.
\]
Splittings of an idempotent form a contractible space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Associativity coherence}

Let
\[
  B\xrightarrow{\Phi}C
  \xrightarrow{\Psi}D
  \xrightarrow{\Theta}E
\]
be a composable string such that
\[
  \Phi,\Psi,\Theta,
  \Psi\Phi,
  \Theta\Psi,
  \Theta\Psi\Phi
\]
are perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

There are two composites
\[
  S_{\Theta\Psi\Phi}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi\otimes_DS_\Theta
\]
obtained by applying the comparisons in the two parenthesizations.

\begin{proposition}[Associativity coherence]
\label{prop:higher-stinespring-associativity-coherence}
The two composites
\[
  S_{\Theta\Psi\Phi}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi\otimes_DS_\Theta
\]
are canonically homotopic.

For four composable admissible coefficient morphisms, the resulting
associativity homotopies satisfy the pentagon coherence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The iterated tensor product is a realization of
\[
  \Theta\Psi\Phi.
\]

Both composites are morphisms from the initial object
\[
  S_{\Theta\Psi\Phi}
\]
to the same realization. The corresponding mapping space is contractible, so
the two maps are canonically homotopic.

For four morphisms, both paths around the pentagon lie in the same
contractible mapping space from the initial realization to the fourfold tensor
product realization. Hence they agree coherently.
\end{proof}

\section{Admissible Strings and the Partial Higher Composition Law}
\label{sec:higher-admissible-strings}

Admissibility is not asserted to be closed under arbitrary composition.
Accordingly, the natural global domain is initially semisimplicial rather than
simplicial.

\begin{definition}[Admissible string]
\label{def:higher-admissible-string}
For \(n\geq1\), an \textbf{admissible string of length \(n\)} is a sequence
\[
  B_0
  \xrightarrow{\Phi_1}
  B_1
  \xrightarrow{\Phi_2}
  \cdots
  \xrightarrow{\Phi_n}
  B_n
\]
of completely square-positive Hermitian coefficient morphisms such that every
nonempty contiguous composite
\[
  \Phi_j\Phi_{j-1}\cdots\Phi_i
\]
is perfectly Stinespring-admissible.
\end{definition}

Let
\[
  \operatorname{Adm}_n
\]
denote the space of admissible strings of length \(n\).

Composition of adjacent morphisms defines the face maps
\[
  d_i:
  \operatorname{Adm}_n
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Adm}_{n-1}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Admissible semisimplicial domain]
\label{prop:higher-admissible-semisimplicial-domain}
The spaces
\[
  \operatorname{Adm}_n,
  \qquad
  n\geq1,
\]
with the face maps \(d_i\), form a semisimplicial space.

The higher Stinespring correspondences and the comparison maps
\[
  \chi
\]
define a coherently associative colax system over this semisimplicial domain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A face map replaces two adjacent coefficient morphisms by their composite.
Every contiguous composite in the resulting shorter string is a contiguous
composite in the original string, hence remains admissible.

The semisimplicial identities follow from associativity of composition of
coefficient morphisms. The coherent colax identities are supplied by
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-stinespring-associativity-coherence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why degeneracies are not automatic]
\label{rem:higher-degeneracies-not-automatic}
The regular \(B\)-\(B\)-bimodule \(B\), anchored at the unit, is always a
perfect realization of
\[
  \operatorname{id}_B.
\]

However, the present Hermitian theory does not imply that this realization is
initial among all perfect realizations of \(\operatorname{id}_B\). Therefore
perfect Stinespring-admissibility of identity coefficients is not asserted
without proof.

Consequently, degeneracy maps are not inserted into
\[
  \operatorname{Adm}_\bullet
\]
by convention.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact Composition and the Pseudofunctorial Locus}
\label{sec:higher-exact-composition}

\begin{definition}[Stinespring-exact pair]
\label{def:higher-stinespring-exact-pair}
A composable pair of perfectly Stinespring-admissible coefficient morphisms
\[
  B\xrightarrow{\Phi}C\xrightarrow{\Psi}D
\]
with admissible composite is \textbf{Stinespring-exact} if
\[
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}:
  S_{\Psi\Phi}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi
\]
is an equivalence.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Equivalent exactness criteria]
\label{prop:higher-exactness-criteria}
The following are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The pair \((\Phi,\Psi)\) is Stinespring-exact.

  \item The tensor-product realization
  \[
    (S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi)
    \boxtimes_C
    (S_\Psi,\xi_\Psi)
  \]
  is minimal.

  \item The support projection
  \[
    p_{\Phi,\Psi}
  \]
  is the identity.

  \item The perfect cyclic tensor support equals the full tensor product:
  \[
    S_\Phi
    \boxtimes_C^{\mathrm{cyc}}
    S_\Psi
    \simeq
    S_\Phi\otimes_CS_\Psi.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-equivalent-minimality-criteria} applied to the
tensor-product realization of \(\Psi\Phi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pseudofunctoriality on an exact unital locus]
\label{thm:higher-stinespring-pseudofunctorial-locus}
Let \(\mathscr C\) be a collection of Hermitian algebras and completely
square-positive coefficient morphisms satisfying the following intrinsic
properties.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every morphism in \(\mathscr C\) is perfectly
  Stinespring-admissible.

  \item Every identity coefficient in \(\mathscr C\) is perfectly
  Stinespring-admissible, and its regular realization is minimal.

  \item The collection is closed under composition.

  \item Every composable pair in \(\mathscr C\) is Stinespring-exact.
\end{enumerate}

Then
\[
  B\longmapsto B,
  \qquad
  \Phi\longmapsto(S_\Phi,\xi_\Phi)
\]
extends to a pseudofunctor from \(\mathscr C\) to the unitary perfect Morita
theory.

Its compositor is the inverse of
\[
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi},
\]
and its unit constraints are the canonical equivalences between the regular
bimodules and the minimal realizations of the identity coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exactness makes every comparison
\[
  \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}
\]
invertible.

The associativity coherence follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-stinespring-associativity-coherence}. The unit
coherences follow from the assumed minimality of the regular identity
realizations and the contractibility of maps out of initial objects.

The pentagon and triangle identities hold because all relevant composites are
maps between the same minimal realizations and belong to contractible mapping
spaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The theorem is conditional, not axiomatic]
\label{rem:higher-pseudofunctor-conditional}
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-stinespring-pseudofunctorial-locus} does not impose its
four conditions on the ambient relative geometry.

It states that any intrinsically identified locus on which those properties
have been proved carries the corresponding pseudofunctorial Stinespring
theory.
\end{remark}

\section{Relative GNS Theory}
\label{sec:higher-relative-gns}

The unit algebra for the relative theory is
\[
  R.
\]

No additional scalar-action axiom is required: every object under
consideration is already an \(R\)-module.

\begin{definition}[Relative Hermitian state]
\label{def:higher-relative-hermitian-state}
A \textbf{relative Hermitian state} on a Hermitian algebra \(B\) is a
completely square-positive Hermitian coefficient morphism
\[
  \varphi:B\to R.
\]

It is \textbf{GNS-admissible} if it is perfectly
Stinespring-admissible.
\end{definition}

For a relative state,
\[
  F_\varphi
  =
  B\otimes_RR
  \simeq
  B.
\]

Under this identification, the canonical Gram form is
\[
  \Gamma_\varphi:
  B\otimes_RB\to R
\]
with
\[
  \Gamma_\varphi(b,b')
  =
  \varphi(\sigma_B(b)b').
\]

\begin{definition}[Higher GNS object]
\label{def:higher-gns-object}
Let
\[
  \varphi:B\to R
\]
be GNS-admissible.

Define
\[
  H_\varphi
  :=
  S_\varphi.
\]

The minimal anchor is denoted
\[
  \Omega_\varphi:
  R\to H_\varphi,
\]
and the left action is denoted
\[
  \pi_\varphi:
  B
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{End}_R(H_\varphi).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Relative higher GNS theorem]
\label{thm:higher-relative-gns}
Let
\[
  \varphi:B\to R
\]
be a GNS-admissible relative Hermitian state.

Then:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(H_\varphi\) is a Hermitian perfect \(R\)-module;

  \item
  \[
    \pi_\varphi:
    B\to\operatorname{End}_R(H_\varphi)
  \]
  is a unital Hermitian algebra representation;

  \item
  \[
    \Omega_\varphi:R\to H_\varphi
  \]
  satisfies
  \[
    \varphi(b)
    \simeq
    \Omega_\varphi^\dagger
    \pi_\varphi(b)
    \Omega_\varphi;
  \]

  \item the pointed representation
  \[
    (H_\varphi,\pi_\varphi,\Omega_\varphi)
  \]
  is initial among perfect Hermitian pointed representations realizing
  \(\varphi\);

  \item its space of minimal pointed unitary equivalences is contractible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-stinespring-realization} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-internal-stinespring-formula} with target algebra
\(R\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalized states]
\label{cor:higher-normalized-gns-vector}
If
\[
  \varphi(\eta_B)
  \simeq
  \eta_R,
\]
then
\[
  \Omega_\varphi^\dagger\Omega_\varphi
  \simeq
  \operatorname{id}_R.
\]

Thus the GNS vector is an isometry.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-normalized-anchor-isometry}.
\end{proof}

\section{Change of Base}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-base-change}

Let
\[
  f:R\to R'
\]
be a morphism of commutative algebra objects.

Extension of scalars gives a strong symmetric monoidal functor
\[
  f^*
  :=
  -\otimes_RR':
  \operatorname{Mod}_R(\mathcal V)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Mod}_{R'}(\mathcal V).
\]

For an \(R\)-algebra \(B\), write
\[
  B_{R'}
  :=
  B\otimes_RR'.
\]

For an \(R\)-linear coefficient morphism
\[
  \Phi:B\to C,
\]
write
\[
  \Phi_{R'}:
  B_{R'}
  \to
  C_{R'}
\]
for its extension of scalars.

\begin{proposition}[Base change of the free orbit and Gram form]
\label{prop:higher-base-change-free-gram}
There are canonical equivalences
\[
  F_{\Phi_{R'}}
  \simeq
  F_\Phi\otimes_RR'
\]
and
\[
  \Gamma_{\Phi_{R'}}
  \simeq
  \Gamma_\Phi\otimes_RR'.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using strong symmetric monoidality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  F_{\Phi_{R'}}
  &=
  B_{R'}\otimes_{R'}C_{R'}
  \\
  &\simeq
  (B\otimes_RR')
  \otimes_{R'}
  (C\otimes_RR')
  \\
  &\simeq
  (B\otimes_RC)\otimes_RR'
  \\
  &=
  F_\Phi\otimes_RR'.
\end{aligned}
\]

The Gram form is a composite of algebra multiplications, involutions,
symmetry maps, and \(\Phi\). A strong symmetric monoidal functor preserves
this composite.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Base change of realized coefficients]
\label{prop:higher-base-change-realized-coefficients}
Let
\[
  (M,\xi)
\]
be an anchored unitary perfect realization of
\[
  \Phi:B\to C.
\]

Then
\[
  (M_{R'},\xi_{R'})
\]
is an anchored unitary perfect realization of
\[
  \Phi_{R'}:
  B_{R'}\to C_{R'}.
\]

In particular, \(\Phi_{R'}\) is completely square-positive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Strong symmetric monoidality preserves dualizable objects, unitary
structures, local adjoints, module actions, and the coefficient formula.

Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_{M_{R'},\xi_{R'}}
  &\simeq
  \Phi_{M,\xi}\otimes_RR'
  \\
  &\simeq
  \Phi\otimes_RR'
  \\
  &=
  \Phi_{R'}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Complete square positivity follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-anchored-coefficients-csp}.
\end{proof}

Now assume that \(\Phi\) is perfectly Stinespring-admissible. Then
\[
  S_\Phi\otimes_RR'
\]
is a realization of \(\Phi_{R'}\).

If \(\Phi_{R'}\) is also perfectly Stinespring-admissible, initiality gives an
anchored isometry
\[
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}:
  S_{\Phi_{R'}}
  \longrightarrow
  S_\Phi\otimes_RR'.
\]

\begin{definition}[Stinespring-exact base change]
\label{def:higher-stinespring-exact-base-change}
Base change along
\[
  f:R\to R'
\]
is \textbf{Stinespring-exact for \(\Phi\)} if
\[
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}
\]
is an equivalence.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Base-change support projection]
\label{prop:higher-base-change-support-projection}
When both \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi_{R'}\) are admissible, the morphism
\[
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}
\]
defines a Hermitian support projection
\[
  p_{\Phi,f}
  :=
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}^\dagger
\]
on
\[
  S_\Phi\otimes_RR'.
\]

Its split image is canonically equivalent to
\[
  S_{\Phi_{R'}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the support-projection construction to the base-changed realization.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No universal exactness claim]
\label{rem:higher-no-universal-base-change-exactness}
The raw orbit bimodule and Gram form commute with base change
unconditionally.

Minimal perfect supports need not commute with base change without a separate
proof. Therefore the comparison
\[
  \kappa_{\Phi,f}
\]
is constructed canonically, while its invertibility is isolated as the
intrinsic Stinespring-exactness property.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Coefficients already presented by correspondences}

\begin{proposition}[Represented coefficients]
\label{prop:higher-represented-coefficients}
Every anchored unitary perfect correspondence

  (M,\xi):B\to C
\]
determines:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a completely square-positive coefficient

  \]

  \item an object

    \in

\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is

\subsection{The identity realization}

For every Hermitian algebra \(B\), the regular \(B\)-\(B\)-bimodule \(B\)
with anchor
\[
  \operatorname{id}_B:B\to B
\]
has coefficient
\[
  \operatorname{id}_B.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Regular realization of the identity]
\label{prop:higher-regular-identity-realization}
There is a canonical object
\[
  (B,\operatorname{id}_B)
  \in
  \operatorname{Real}_R(\operatorname{id}_B).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_{B,\operatorname{id}_B}(b)
  &=
  \operatorname{id}_B^\dagger
  \lambda_B(b)
  \operatorname{id}_B
  \\
  &\simeq
  b.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:higher-identity-minimality-boundary}
The regular identity realization exists without qualification.

Its minimality is a separate theorem and is not inferred solely from
nondegeneracy of the Hermitian pairing. In indefinite Hermitian settings,
vanishing self-pairing does not force a vector to vanish. Thus the usual
analytic multiplicative-domain argument cannot be imported without proof.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Comparison with an ordinary separated quotient}

Suppose the theory is specialized to an ordinary additive Hermitian setting
in which the canonical Gram module admits a radical
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}(F_\Phi)
\]
and a separated quotient
\[
  \operatorname{Sep}(F_\Phi)
  :=
  F_\Phi/\operatorname{Rad}(F_\Phi).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Recovery from a universal separated quotient]
\label{prop:higher-recovery-separated-quotient}
Assume:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\operatorname{Sep}(F_\Phi)\) is unitary and right-perfect;

  \item every perfect Gram factorization
  \[
    q:F_\Phi\to M
  \]
  kills
  \[
    \operatorname{Rad}(F_\Phi);
  \]

  \item the induced map
  \[
    \operatorname{Sep}(F_\Phi)\to M
  \]
  is an isometry.
\end{enumerate}

Then \(\Phi\) is perfectly Stinespring-admissible and
\[
  S_\Phi
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Sep}(F_\Phi).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient map
\[
  F_\Phi\to\operatorname{Sep}(F_\Phi)
\]
is a perfect Gram factorization.

By the second assumption, every other perfect Gram factorization factors
through it. By the third assumption, the factorization is a morphism in
\[
  \operatorname{GramFact}_R(\Phi).
\]

The factorization is unique because the quotient map is an epimorphism in the
ordinary additive category. Hence the separated quotient is initial.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:higher-recovery-assumptions-not-ambient}
The three assumptions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-recovery-separated-quotient} are hypotheses of a
comparison theorem for a particular ordinary specialization. They are not
standing assumptions of the higher theory.
\end{remark}

\section{Boundary of the Theory}
\label{sec:higher-stinespring-boundary}

The constructions and theorems above do not assert any of the following.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item They do not assert that every completely square-positive coefficient
  morphism is perfectly Stinespring-admissible.

  \item They do not assert that every canonical Gram form admits a radical
  quotient internal to the chosen stable infinity-category.

  \item They do not assert that every realization contains a minimal
  realization unless the coefficient is admissible.

  \item They do not assert that identity coefficients are always admissible.

  \item They do not assert that admissible coefficients are closed under
  arbitrary composition.

  \item They do not assert that the tensor product of two minimal
  realizations is minimal.

  \item They do not assert that every colax comparison
  \[
    \chi_{\Phi,\Psi}
  \]
  is an equivalence.

  \item They do not assert that minimal Stinespring supports commute with
  every base change.

  \item They do not introduce an analytic norm completion.

  \item They do not introduce a positive cone or an order relation.

  \item They do not introduce a global dagger on the entire ambient
  infinity-category.

  \item They do not prove a Radon--Nikodym theorem or a dominance theorem.
\end{enumerate}

These are not omitted assumptions. They are statements whose proofs would
require additional constructions not supplied by the standing relative
Hermitian Morita geometry.
